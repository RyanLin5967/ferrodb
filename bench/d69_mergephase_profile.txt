D69-REOPEN — WHERE A MERGE ACTUALLY SPENDS ITS TIME. The profile that overturned D68's mechanism.

[HERE] `sample` over 30 s of `D68_SIZES=16000 D68_MERGES=3000`, sampling started 75 s after launch
so the BUILD phase is excluded. Raw profile beside this file as d69_mergephase_profile.sample.txt.

  executor::run                          99.9%
  wal::txn::TxnManager::commit           99.4%
  wal::log::WalManager::flush            98.2%   <-- the merge's cost
  wal::log::pwrite_all                   14.7%
  TxnManager::checkpoint_locked           2.0%
  execution::insert::Insert::execute      0.3%   <-- proof the build is excluded
  ...no scan frame appears at all.

⛔ **THIS CONTRADICTS D68'S STATED MECHANISM.** D68 attributed the 1.87 us/row slope to two full
table scans (`evaluate_merge` and `fingerprint_tables`). Those scans were REAL — they are in the
source at 26627a7 and D69 removed them — but they are NOT where the time goes. A merge is
FSYNC-BOUND, not scan-bound, and removing the scans left the curve unchanged
(`d69_verify_curve.txt`: 32.023 ms after against 32.003 ms before at 16,000 rows).

So the slope against table size is real and reproducible, and its CAUSE is still unknown. D68
identified two genuine scans and wrongly concluded they were the cost; that is a lesson about
reading a profile aggregate rather than a call tree, and about a mechanism that is plausible,
verified present in the source, and still not the answer.

⚠ MY FIRST ATTEMPT AT THIS PROFILE WAS INVALID and is recorded so it is not repeated: with
`D68_MERGES=400` at 16,000 rows, 97.8% of samples were also in `WalManager::flush` — but that was
the BUILD phase (16,000 INSERTs, each with a WAL commit), not the merges. `Insert::execute` at
0.3% in this run is what establishes the build is excluded; the earlier run had no such check.

WHAT IS STILL OPEN, stated as two candidates rather than one guess:
  (a) the fsync itself scales with the database/dirty-page volume, so a bigger table costs more per
      commit even for identical work; or
  (b) a merge writes more WAL on a bigger table.
The measurement for (b) is bytes appended per merge — under way, with the caveat that the log is
TRUNCATED at checkpoints so a file-size delta undercounts and can even go negative (the 1,000-row
point reported the log SHRINKING, 42,273 -> 17,027).
The orthogonal measurement is delta at FIXED table size: if cost is flat in delta, it is a
per-merge constant that depends on the TABLE, not on the work — which points at (a).
