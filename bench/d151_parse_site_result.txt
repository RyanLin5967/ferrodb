WHAT MAKES THIS CHECKABLE RATHER THAN PLAUSIBLE
-----------------------------------------------
1. THE BEFORE ARM REPRODUCES A NUMBER I DID NOT PRODUCE. bf1834a banked 33/132/528/2112 and
   24/96/384/1536 on base ee24331. My BEFORE arm, on base 2a0a8fc — 35 commits later — returned
   the same sixteen integers. The disagreement that would have voided everything downstream did
   not happen, and it was checked FIRST.
2. TWO CONTROLS, BOTH STILL ZERO. Arm B never constructs a `ServerContext`; arm A0's `SELECT 1`
   is answered by `session::parse` as a liveness probe and never reaches either catalog site. A
   change that moved either would be reaching somewhere it has no business reaching.
3. THE RESIDUAL IS ZERO PER SITE, NOT JUST IN TOTAL. bf1834a attributed every BEFORE acquisition
   to three sites: (i) `parse_one`, (ii) `read_catalog`'s stale-snapshot refresh, (iii) the
   exclusive execute arm. D151 removes (i) and only (i), so:
     A2 after = (ii) + (iii) = 2 per fork.                        Measured 2.000, at every T.
     A1 after = 2 statements x (iii) + (ii) once per connection
              = (16 x 1 + 1) per connection = 17T.                Measured 17T, at every T.
   The per-fork constants do not move by a thousandth from T=1 to T=64, before or after.

⚠ WHAT THIS MEASUREMENT DOES NOT SHOW, AND THE PRE-REGISTRATION SAID TO SAY SO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
P3 predicted the drop might EXCEED the per-statement amount at T>1, because a parse-time
announcement drains readers on other connections and pushes them onto the exclusive execute path,
where they announce in turn. **That extra drop is exactly zero here, and the reason is the
harness, not the change.** Every statement in arms A1 and A2 is `BEGIN AGENT SESSION` or
`ABANDON`; neither is a read, so `try_run_read` answers `None` for both and they take the
exclusive path regardless. There was never a shared-path read in flight for a parse-time
announcement to stand down. **P3 was untestable on this harness, not falsified**, and this file
claims nothing about it. Measuring it needs a concurrent arm with real readers, which E.6 does
not have and which this row did not build.

The code fact that is NOT a measurement, stated as such: a statement that only reads now takes
the exclusive lock at neither parse nor execute. Before D151 it took it once, at parse, however
pure a reader it was. That is visible in the diff; it is not what the table above measures,
because this harness runs no such statement outside the A0 control.
