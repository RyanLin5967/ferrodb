The two runs disagree by 34% at N=100,000 and 46% at N=250,000, and they disagree about the
DIRECTION. Run 2's apparent doubling is not evidence of O(N): run 1 measured the same two sizes
and found flat. ⛔ NOTHING about reopen's complexity class can be extracted from this, and
quoting run 2's 2x would be reporting this machine's load as a property of the catalog.

THE FAULT IS THE INSTRUMENT. `reopen_ms` is a SINGLE wall-clock sample per checkpoint on a shared,
heavily loaded box. One sample cannot distinguish a real cost from a scheduling artifact, which is
the same lesson as [[a-benchmark-harness-can-be-the-wall]] in a different guise — and this file's
own pre-registration anticipated it ("noisy upward; a single high cell is load until the shape says
otherwise"). It was right, and one sample per point was not enough to act on it.

WHAT THE NEXT RUN MUST CHANGE, both of them, before any reopen number is quoted again:
  1. REPEAT the reopen k times per checkpoint and report MEDIAN and MIN, not one sample. The MIN
     is the least load-contaminated estimate available — a reopen cannot be made faster by
     contention, only slower — so a flat MIN column is much stronger evidence than a flat single
     sample, and a climbing MIN is much harder to dismiss.
  2. SHRINK THE SPAN TO WHAT THE DISK ALLOWS. 10k..250k is a 25x span for ~1 GB of data, which
     fits inside the floor with room to spare, and 25x across five points answers the
     complexity-class question far better than two points at 10^6 ever could
     ([[measure-the-slope-not-the-ratio]]). Extending to 10^6 is then a separate, larger run for
     when the machine is quiet.

WHAT IS NOT IN DOUBT: len B/branch reads 4096 at both checkpoints in both runs, agreeing with
D61's five-point decade. The space result stands; only reopen is unmeasured.
