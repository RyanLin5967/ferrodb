====================================================================================
VERDICT
====================================================================================
Option (c) SURVIVES, and it closes check 4. But the row's own framing of it is wrong, and the
correction is the finding:

  ** THE ROOT CELL DOES NOT NEED TO BE MADE DURABLE. IT IS THE SECOND COPY, NOT THE FIRST. **

There is already exactly ONE durable home for a root, it is already at a fixed location, and
the circularity the brief asks about is already solved. What (c) has to do is not "add
durability to the cell" but "stop having two values", and the cheapest way to do that needs no
new file, no new page, no new format and no new bootstrap.

Recommended form, named (c3) below: make `Catalog::persist()` — which is ALREADY `&self` —
read the live cell instead of the record field, and have the DML tail call it (conditionally)
instead of `sync_roots`. The diff is contained; it needs one new short mutex private to
`persist`, and that mutex provably does not recreate check 4's deadlock (argument in section 6).

The textbook end-state, (c5) "the root page id never moves", is STRICTLY BETTER on the D1 axis
and is what SQLite and InnoDB do — but it invalidates D53's mechanism and both of D53's pinning
tests, and it needs a new correctness argument inside the B-link descent. That is its own row,
not part of W4. Stated, not recommended for this lane.

1. WHERE THE ROOT CELL LIVES TODAY — the accounting, verified
====================================================================================
There are TWO copies of every tree root, plus a reconciler between them.

COPY 1 — DURABLE.  `TableEntry::primary_index_root`, `IndexInfo::root_page_id`,
  `FullTextIndexInfo::root_page_id`, serialized onto the catalog page chain.
  - The chain head is `Catalog::first_catalog_page_id`, and `Catalog::create`
    (catalog.rs:65-76) comments it "= 1 on a fresh DB"; `cli.rs:67` opens with the constant
    `FIRST_CATALOG_PAGE_ID`.
    => A FIXED LOCATION. The circularity the brief warns about is already answered, and (c)
    does not disturb it.
  - Byte layout: catalog_page.rs:65 header, then per entry
    `name_len|name|first_directory_page_id|primary_index_root|time_travel_root|...`
    (serialize at catalog_page.rs:74-100, deserialize at :190-279).
  - SOLE WRITER of catalog-page bytes, verified by grepping every assignment to `frame.data`
    in catalog.rs: :74 (`create`), :478 (stamping a NEW chain page), :492 (`persist`).
    Nothing else in src/ writes a catalog page. That is the property (c) must preserve, and
    (c3) preserves it exactly.

COPY 2 — IN MEMORY, AND AUTHORITATIVE AT RUNTIME.  `Catalog::roots:
  HashMap<(String, Option<String>), Arc<AtomicU32>>` (catalog.rs:56).
  Its own doc: "**Shared root cells, one per tree.** Deliberately NOT serialized — it is
  derived from `tables` and rebuilt by [`Catalog::sync_root_cells`]" (catalog.rs:44-46).
  - Seeded from COPY 1 at load (`sync_root_cells`, catalog.rs:112-131; call at :534).
  - Written on the hot path in exactly one place: `index.rs:593`,
    `self.root_page_id.store(new_page_id, Ordering::Release)` inside `insert_into_parent`,
    on `&self`, holding page write latches, with no catalog in scope at all.
  - Handed to statements by `Catalog::root_cell(&self, ..)` (catalog.rs:89-91) — a plain hash
    lookup on `&Catalog`, no lock — and consumed by `plan::open_table` (plan.rs:109-134),
    `optimizer.rs:63-81`, `fulltext_search.rs:250`.

RECONCILER.  `sync_roots` (executor.rs:495-510) and `sync_fulltext_roots` (executor.rs:522-532),
  called at the tail of all three DML writers:
    insert.rs:168-169 · update.rs:156-157 · delete.rs:69
  Each loads COPY 2, compares with COPY 1, and calls `update_primary_root` /
  `update_index_root` / `update_fulltext_root` only when they differ.

2. THE FINDING THAT MAKES (c) EASY, AND IT SHARPENS W4's PREMISE
====================================================================================
`Catalog::persist()` is declared `pub fn persist(&self)` — catalog.rs:432. NOT `&mut self`.

=> THE DURABLE WRITE HAS NEVER NEEDED `&mut Catalog`.

What needs `&mut` is the three-line prologue in `update_primary_root` (catalog.rs:365-376):
    let entry = self.tables.get_mut(table)?;   // <-- the ONLY reason for &mut
    entry.primary_index_root = new_root;
    self.persist()?;                            // &self
    if let Some(cell) = self.roots.get(..) { cell.store(new_root, Release); }

So the exclusive, whole-statement, every-connection-waits catalog mutex is held at the DML tail
**to mutate an in-memory mirror of a value that no runtime reader consults.** Check 1 already
established the second half of that sentence (every runtime reader of `primary_index_root` is a
`None =>` fallback arm behind D53's shared cell). This pass adds the first half: the mirror is
write-mostly. Its readers are `persist()` (which re-serializes it), `rebuild_indexes`
(recovery.rs:235/246/272, which OVERWRITES it), `alter.rs`, and the fallback arms.

=> W4's tail dependency on `&mut Catalog` is not a durability requirement. It is an artifact of
keeping a mutable second copy. That is the D1 lesson pointing at D53's own cell.

3. THE FOUR SHAPES OF (c), DEFLATED IN ORDER
====================================================================================
(c1) WRITE-THROUGH CELL PATCHING THE CATALOG PAGE IN PLACE.
  `Arc<AtomicU32>` becomes `Arc<DurableRoot{ atomic, page_id, byte_offset, pool }>`; `store`
  also patches 4 bytes at `byte_offset`.
  Checks that pass: the field sits at a computable offset (catalog_page.rs:95-97); the page
  carries a `checksum` field (catalog_page.rs:14) that is copied through serialize/deserialize
  and NEVER COMPUTED OR VERIFIED anywhere in src/, so an in-place patch would not invalidate it.
  (Recorded as an observation about the checksum, not a recommendation.)
  WHY IT DIES: it puts the catalog-page byte layout in TWO places — `CatalogPage::serialize`
  and the patcher — and the second one fails SILENTLY in the direction that looks like success.
  A layout change writes 4 bytes into the middle of a column name and you find out on reopen.
  That is a second mechanism at the byte level, which is the D1 objection one layer down.
  REJECTED.

(c2) A NEW FIXED "ROOTS PAGE", i.e. the `TableBranchCatalog::publish_root` pattern
  (branch/table_catalog.rs:359-375) generalised. That function is real prior art in this repo
  and is exactly (c) done once: it loads the tree's live root, compares against a published
  value with an `AtomicU32::swap`, writes a fixed header page only when it moved, and it is
  `&self`. Its sibling doc states the circularity answer outright (branch/table_catalog.rs:379):
  "The root page id is the **one** value that cannot live in the tree, because reading the tree
  requires it."
  WHY IT DOES NOT TRANSFER: `TableBranchCatalog` has ONE tree and ONE root. The SQL catalog has
  N roots over a set that changes with DDL, so a roots page needs slot allocation, slot reuse on
  DROP, and compaction. And to avoid being a second copy, `TableEntry`'s root fields have to be
  DELETED — which changes the catalog page format, `wal/recovery.rs`'s rebuild, `alter.rs`, the
  `None =>` fallbacks, and the catalog image carried in `consensus/snapshot.rs`.
  This is "add a new durable file for roots". It is what D1's lesson says not to do, and the
  deflation the brief asked for is the one that kills it. REJECTED.

(c3) *** MAKE `persist()` READ THE CELL. *** <- RECOMMENDED
  One value, one durable location, one writer of that location.
  - `Catalog::persist()` already walks `self.tables` and clones each `TableEntry` into the page
    (catalog.rs:440-461). Change: before `add_entry`, overwrite the clone's root fields from
    `self.roots` — `self.root_cell(name, None)`, plus one per secondary and full-text index.
    `persist` is `&self` and `root_cell` is `&self`, so no signature changes.
  - `sync_roots` / `sync_fulltext_roots` are replaced by ONE `&Catalog` function that compares
    each live cell against the stored record and calls `persist()` iff any differ — the same
    conditional check 1 measured, in the same place, on the same rare branch.
  - `TableEntry::primary_index_root` stops being an independently-mutated value and becomes what
    it already is in practice: the seed `sync_root_cells` reads at load, and a field `persist`
    writes. `update_primary_root` and friends stay for ALTER/DROP/tests, which legitimately hold
    `&mut`.
  RESULT: the DML tail takes `&Catalog`. `sync_roots` leaves the exclusive path. No new file, no
  new page, no new format, no new bootstrap, and the catalog page still has exactly one writer.

(c5) MAKE THE ROOT PAGE ID CONSTANT — the standard answer, stated because rule 1b demands it.
  On a root split, copy the old root's contents into a freshly allocated page (which becomes the
  left child) and rewrite the SAME page id as the new internal root. SQLite's `balanceDeeper`;
  InnoDB records a fixed root page number in its data dictionary for the same reason.
  Then COPY 1 is never stale, because it never changes after CREATE. `sync_roots`,
  `update_*_root`, `Catalog::roots` and `sync_root_cells` all become unnecessary, and the cost
  is one page alloc + one page copy per ROOT split (O(log_F N) over a table's entire life), with
  ZERO catalog interaction ever.
  WHY IT IS NOT THIS ROW: it is B+tree surgery under the B-link optimistic descent
  (index.rs:240-260), the project's most delicate invariant, and it falsifies D53 by
  construction. `tests/d53_shared_root_cell.rs:45` inserts "until its root page id changes" —
  under (c5) that loop never terminates. `tests/d53_private_root_allowlist.rs` pins the
  `root_cell`+`open_shared` shape that (c5) makes pointless. Those are not stale assertions to
  be quieted; they pin a mechanism (c5) deliberately removes, which is a contract change needing
  its own row and its own argument. NOT bundled into W4.

4. CIRCULARITY CHECK — the one the brief demanded
====================================================================================
"Reopen must find the roots before it can read anything, so whatever holds them must be
reachable from a fixed location."
Under (c3): the roots stay exactly where they are — inside `TableEntry` on the catalog page
chain whose head is the constant page 1. Reopen is `Catalog::open(bp, FIRST_CATALOG_PAGE_ID)`
-> `load()` -> `sync_root_cells()` (catalog.rs:534), unchanged, byte for byte the same path.
(c3) ADDS NO BOOTSTRAP. PASSES trivially, and that is the main reason to prefer it over (c2),
which would have had to invent one.

5. D1 CHECK — "is this a second mechanism?"
====================================================================================
D1's lesson is that the branch catalog stopped being a second mechanism and became a system
table so there would not be two. Applied here honestly:

  TODAY there are already two copies of the root and a reconciler between them. D53 created the
  second one (for a good reason: the root-split retry was dormant without it) and `sync_roots`
  is the tax. W4's check 4 deadlock is that tax coming due on the shared path.

  (c3) REMOVES a copy rather than adding one. After it, the cell is the only mutable root value
  in the process and the catalog page is the only durable one, written by the only function that
  has ever written it. The count of mechanisms goes 2 -> 1.

  (c1) and (c2) both go 2 -> 2-or-3 and are rejected on exactly this axis.

6. DOES (c3) REINTRODUCE CHECK 4's DEADLOCK? NO — and here is the argument
====================================================================================
Check 4 (extended.rs:333-335): "a writer draining would wait for this slot while this reader
waited for the mutex." The cycle needs someone holding a READ SLOT to block on the CATALOG
MUTEX.

(c3)'s tail takes no catalog mutex. It calls `persist()`, which today takes no lock at all. It
DOES need one new short lock — see section 7 — call it `persist_lock`, a `Mutex<()>` inside
`Catalog`, taken and released entirely inside `persist()`.

Lock-order graph, all edges:
  - exclusive path: catalog mutex -> persist_lock   (a DDL persists while holding the mutex)
  - shared path:    read slot     -> persist_lock   (a splitting DML persists while holding its
                                                     pass)
  - drain_readers:  catalog mutex -> waits on read slots
  - persist_lock holder: waits on NOTHING RELEVANT. Audited rather than assumed — every lock-ish
    call in the body of `persist` (catalog.rs:432-520) is a buffer-pool call and nothing else:
    `fetch_page`, `frames[i].read()`, `frame_write`, `unpin_page`, `new_page`, and the orphan
    `deallocate`. No catalog mutex, no read slot, no B+tree latch.
Because persist_lock has out-degree zero it is a sink, so no cycle exists. A DML holding a slot
can block behind another persist, and that persist always completes. PASSES.
  THE ONE EDGE THAT WOULD BREAK THIS, stated so it is checked and not rediscovered: persist ->
  buffer-pool page-table lock (via `new_page`/`deallocate`). A cycle would need some path that
  holds the pool's page-table lock and then blocks on persist_lock. Nothing can today, because
  persist_lock is new and `persist` is its only acquirer. Anyone who adds a second acquirer
  inherits that obligation, and it belongs in the lock's own doc comment.

Contrast with option (a), which died because it PUT THE TOKEN DOWN. (c3) never drops the pass,
so the DDL exclusion the barrier provides holds for the whole statement — which is also why
`sync_roots`' `ok_or(KeyNotFound)` (executor.rs:497) stays a can't-happen under (c3). Do not
"fix" that `ok_or`; under (c3) it remains the tell, and it remains unreachable.

7. THE ONE REAL COST (c3) BUYS — an obligation, not a footnote
====================================================================================
`persist()` is `&self` but has never actually run concurrently: every caller today reaches it
from a `&mut self` method or from recovery. (c3) makes it a genuinely concurrent path, and it
is not safe as written:

  (i) LOST UPDATE OF A ROOT. Two shared-path persists interleave; the one that read an older
      cell value finishes LAST and writes a stale root. That is the exact corruption
      `sync_roots` exists to prevent.
  (ii) ORPHAN-PAGE DOUBLE FREE. `persist` shrinks the chain by unlinking and deallocating the
      orphan tail (catalog.rs:486 onward). Two concurrent persists can walk and free the same
      orphan list twice. This is the less obvious of the two and the more damaging.

FIX, and it is one lock, not a new mechanism: a `persist_lock: Mutex<()>` taken as the first
statement of `persist()`, WITH THE CELL READS DONE INSIDE IT. That is sufficient, not merely
necessary:
  - split S stores into its cell at t; S then calls persist, which serializes behind P.
  - if P's in-lock cell read happens after t, P captured S's value.
  - if before t, P wrote the older value, and S's own persist runs strictly after P releases and
    writes the newer one.
  Either way the last write is the live value. Both hazards close.

CRASH WINDOW: unchanged. Today a split stores the cell and the statement's `sync_roots`
persists it; with (c3) a split stores the cell and the statement's tail persists it. Same
statement, same window, no regression.

WRITE VOLUME: LOWER than today, not higher. I drafted this paragraph as "more work per root
split" and it was wrong — `update_primary_root` ALREADY calls `self.persist()` (catalog.rs:368),
and so do `update_index_root` (:381) and `update_fulltext_root` (:395). So a statement that moves
a primary root and two secondary roots persists the whole chain THREE times today. (c3) compares
first and persists ONCE. Correcting my own draft here rather than shipping the inflated claim.

8. WHAT (c3) DOES AND DOES NOT DISSOLVE
====================================================================================
CHECK 4 (the rare root write / deadlock):  DISSOLVED. No path takes the catalog mutex while
  holding a read slot; the tail is `&Catalog` throughout.
CHECK 3 (stand-down rate):  NOT DISSOLVED. A shared-path DML still calls `begin_read` and still
  stands down whenever a writer is announced, whatever the tail does. (c3) changes nothing here;
  w4-standdown-count still bounds the payoff.
  => The brief's claim that "(c) makes checks 3 and 4 BOTH dissolve" is HALF FALSE. It is check
  4 only. SCALE-DESIGN's own later entry ("CHECK 3 IS DISPATCHED, AND IT WAS NEVER BLOCKED ON A
  QUIET BOX") already caught this; recording it here so the two do not disagree, and so the
  earlier sentence is not quoted forward on its own.
CHECK 2 (`bump_table_version`):  unaffected. After (c3) it is the LAST remaining `&mut Catalog`
  user in `Modify::execute`, which matches D51's compiler probe finding exactly two write sites.
  Making it an atomic (checks 2a/2b: the bump lands inside the pass, and MERGE reaches
  `drain_readers` unconditionally at extended.rs:357) is the other half of the tail, and it is
  independent of this row.

10. BY-PRODUCTS FOUND WHILE READING, worth the next reader's time
====================================================================================
 - `CatalogPage::checksum` (catalog_page.rs:14, written at :84, read at :199) is never computed
   and never verified anywhere in src/. A catalog page is not checksum-protected. Observation
   only; it is also what makes (c1) technically possible, and (c1) is still rejected.
 - `persist()` frees orphan catalog pages (catalog.rs:486 onward). Anything that makes `persist`
   concurrent inherits a double-free, not just a lost update.
 - `recover()` returns `Ok(true)` whenever the WAL holds ANY record (recovery.rs:17-19, :124),
   and `checkpoint_locked` re-appends every DDL record after truncating (txn.rs:857-864 via
   `replay_schema`, txn.rs:791-800). So a process that ran DDL leaves a non-empty WAL at exit and
   the next open runs `rebuild_indexes`, which frees and rebuilds every tree from the heap. A
   process that ran only DML leaves `schema_log` empty, truncates to nothing, and the next open
   TRUSTS the recorded root.
   I checked this precisely because I expected to be able to argue that the recorded root never
   matters — that `rebuild_indexes` makes it dead weight. IT IS NOT DEAD WEIGHT: the DML-only
   process is exactly the window `sync_roots` protects, and it is the common one for a server.
   The executor's durability claim at executor.rs:514-517 stands. Premise checked and NOT
   falsified; recorded because the negative was the point of checking.
