The batch SATURATES, at a value this sweep measures: C ≈ 17.3.

  and at T=64 the two direct runs are 17.08 and 17.28 — **1.2% apart**, twenty minutes

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. AT THE LAYER PRODUCTION RUNS, THERE IS NO GROUP COMMIT AT ALL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

`f/sync` is **1.00 at every T from 1 to 128** in arm P, with `syncs` exactly equal to
`forks` in every cell — 40/40, 80/80, … 5120/5120. Not approximately: exactly. Every
`BEGIN AGENT SESSION` over the wire pays a private fsync and shares it with nobody, at
128 concurrent connections as at one.

⭐ **THE NEGATIVE IS FALSIFIED, NOT ASSUMED.** A flat 1.00 read alone is indistinguishable
from a harness that could never have shown anything else. Arm D is the control: it forks
on the rig's OWN `Arc<TableBranchCatalog>`, reads the SAME `syncs_issued()`, runs in the
same process with a pgwire server listening throughout, and differs from P and S only in
not going through the socket. It rises **1.00 → 17.12, a factor of 17.1**, across the
same T values on which P never leaves 1.00. ⇒ The instrument can see a batch. The wire
layer has none.

Arm S answers the other objection — that arm P's threads spend their time connecting
rather than queueing, so few are ever inside `wait_durable` together. It holds one
connection per thread and returns to the fork immediately. It reads **exactly 0.20 at
every T**, which is 1/5: five catalog fsyncs per BEGIN+ABANDON cycle, and just as flat.
⇒ The connection shape is not the reason either.

The mechanism is the second alternative outcome 2 names — *"an fsync that is not the
serialization point at all"*. `ServerContext::catalog()` is taken outermost for the
duration of one statement; `try_run_read` admits only EXPLAIN and non-`AS OF` SELECT, and
the fork statement is not among them. So no two forks are ever concurrently inside
`wait_durable`, and there is never a group to commit. This is an independent, load-immune
confirmation of what E.6 measured with lock counts and what W4 reads off the source.

⇒ ⭐ THE GROUP-COMMIT MECHANISM THE 21×/1.9× PAIR IS ABOUT IS INVISIBLE TO ANYTHING THAT
  CONNECTS OVER THE WIRE.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. WHAT THIS LICENSES — AND WHAT IT DOES NOT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

⛔ **STAGED GROUP COMMIT IS NOT LICENSED BY THIS RESULT AND IS NOT PROPOSED ON IT.**
The entry pre-wrote that fix for outcome 1, with its prior art named: PostgreSQL's
`commit_delay`/`commit_siblings`; MySQL 5.6 / MariaDB binlog group commit split into
flush/sync/commit stages (Kristian Nielsen); ARIES (Mohan et al., TODS 1992); RocksDB's
write-batch leader. Outcome 1 did not fire. Staging lifts the batch from `T/2` toward `T`
only where `T/2` is the binding term — `T ≤ 16` here — and at and above the knee the
batch is capped at C by the serial section, so staging buys nothing at the operating
point. At the pgwire layer it buys nothing at any T, because there is no group to stage.

✅ What the counts do license, both of them engineering this project has already named:

  (a) DIRECT LAYER — the lever is `C`, the number of forks that can pass through
      `logical` while one fsync runs. Shortening that section is the only thing that
      moves the batch above the knee. Not new: `bench/fork_concurrency_after.txt` already
      concluded *"the batch size is not set by the disk at all — it is set by HOW MANY
      FORKS CAN GET THROUGH `logical` WHILE ONE FSYNC RUNS"*, from a T=64-vs-T=256 pair.
      This sweep confirms it across eight values of T, locates the knee at `T ≈ 2C`, and
      reproduces C three times in two fixtures.

  (b) PGWIRE LAYER — the lever is the outer statement lock, and the change is already
      written down as W4 falsifier 3: *"Does the fork statement (`BEGIN AGENT SESSION`)
      need `&mut Catalog` at all? It is not DML. If it does not, it may be admissible to
      `try_run_read` independently of DML — a much smaller change that directly answers
      E.6."* Until something of that shape lands, no work on `group_commit.rs` changes a
      single number a client can observe.

⇒ ⭐ THE FINDING IS THE CORRECTION, NOT A MECHANISM. The most-cited quantity in this
  project is a batch that saturates at ~17.3 and a wire layer where it is 1.00, and
  candidates have been designed against `T/D` as though `T` were in the numerator. It is
  — but only below `T ≈ 32`, and never at all through pgwire.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. SUBORDINATE CROSS-CHECK — NOT EVIDENCE FOR THE OUTCOME
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The outcome above rests on counts alone. This paragraph is a consistency check and is
labelled as one, because the box is shared and every duration on it is an upper bound.

If `C = D/S`, the single-thread per-fork cost is `D + S` and the saturated per-fork cost
is `S`. From rep 1 of the direct sweep above: 3.811 ms at T=1 and 0.191 ms at T=128 give
`S ≈ 0.191`, `D ≈ 3.620`, `D/S ≈ 19.0`, against a measured batch of 17.3 — **10% apart,
and HIGH**, which is the direction the instrument predicts: both durations are upper
bounds on a shared box, and the T=1 cost carries per-fork scheduling that `D` does not,
so this estimate of `D` is inflated and `D/S` with it. It is consistent with `C = D/S`
at the precision two upper-bound durations can support, and it is not offered as proof
of it. No throughput figure from this sweep is quoted anywhere above.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. WHAT THIS RUN CANNOT SAY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

- **It does not test the stubbed ladder.** L1–L3 exist only on `CEILING-park-or-structure`,
  a never-merge scaffold on a divergent base. Whether `f/sync` stays pinned at `T/2` up to
  T=128 on those rungs is untested here. It would not change the finding: those rungs are
  not a database, and §2 shows the real rung obeys `T/2` below its own knee anyway.
- **The pgwire arms are one rep**, not three. They do not need more: every cell is an
  exact integer identity (`syncs == forks`, `syncs == 5 × forks`), not a noisy average.
- **`FERRODB_PARENT_ARENAS=0`**, so the direct harness cannot see D7's arena scan. That
  keeps `S` at its smallest and `C = D/S` at its largest, which is the direction that
  favours the hypothesis under test — the batch saturates anyway.

================================================================================
8. ⭐ THE LOAD-BEARING PREMISE — STATED AS ONE SENTENCE, AND RE-CHECKED AT MAIN'S
   CURRENT HEAD RATHER THAN AT THIS BRANCH'S BASE.
================================================================================

Three sibling lanes came back NOT LANDABLE on the same day for a false premise rather
than a wrong number — one of them because a premise true when written was falsified by a
merge 2.5 hours later. Everything above rests on one premise of exactly that kind, so it
is written here to be checked rather than assumed.

⭐ **THE PREMISE.** `CommitGroup::wait_durable` (`src/branch/group_commit.rs`) is
single-stage commit-when-ready: the leader sets `syncing`, reads `let target =
st.requested;` while still holding the state lock and before it calls `sync()`, and
applies no size cap and no accumulation delay — so one fsync covers exactly the tickets
that existed at the instant it began, and a ticket handed out during a sync waits a whole
further round.

That is what makes `throughput = min(1/S, T/D)` and the `f/sync ≈ T/2` race prediction
mean anything at all. If it is false, every number above is computed on top of a fiction.

**CHECKED 2026-09-22T11:45Z against `main` at `98a44ef`** — not against `2bc80fd`, which
is where this branch was cut and which main has since moved past several times:

    git rev-parse 2bc80fd:src/branch/group_commit.rs  -> 81b8b5dfcf8b0a5c94849c7ea4d185a7620a8275
    git rev-parse 98a44ef:src/branch/group_commit.rs  -> 81b8b5dfcf8b0a5c94849c7ea4d185a7620a8275
    git rev-parse main:src/branch/group_commit.rs     -> 81b8b5dfcf8b0a5c94849c7ea4d185a7620a8275

⇒ **Byte-identical.** `src/branch/table_catalog.rs`, which holds the only production call
sites, is likewise unchanged across that span (`git diff --stat 2bc80fd..main --` on both
files is empty). The premise is TRUE AT HEAD, and the measurement was taken on the same
blob production runs.

**The symbols, not line numbers** — line numbers drift, and correcting a right one with a
wrong one has already cost this project once:

  - `src/branch/group_commit.rs` :: `CommitGroup::wait_durable` — reads `target` under the
    state lock, immediately after setting `syncing` and immediately before `drop(st)`.
  - `src/branch/group_commit.rs` :: `CommitGroup::ticket` — the only way `requested` moves.
  - `src/branch/table_catalog.rs` :: `TableBranchCatalog::stage` — the ONLY production
    caller of `ticket()`; takes it after `publish_root()`, under the logical lock.
  - `src/branch/table_catalog.rs` :: `TableBranchCatalog::durable` — the ONLY production
    caller of `wait_durable`; supplies `flush_all` + `disk_manager.sync()` as the fsync.

⇒ Enumerated from the callers, not assumed: at `main` there are exactly **four** callers
  of `ticket()` — three of them in this file's own `#[cfg(test)]` module — and exactly
  **one** caller of `wait_durable` outside the module. There is no second commit path for
  the premise to be false on.

⛔ **ONE PRECISION, SO NOBODY "CORRECTS" THIS PREMISE WRONGLY LATER.** The phrase "no
timer" is right about batching and wrong as a literal reading of the file: `wait_durable`
contains `wait_timeout(st, Duration::from_millis(50))`. That is a FOLLOWER's bounded park,
present so a lost wakeup cannot hang a writer for the life of the process, and the file
says so in as many words. It is **not** a leader delaying its flush to collect more
tickets — which is precisely what PostgreSQL's `commit_delay` is, and precisely what this
code does not have. A reader who greps `Duration::` here will find it; the premise
survives, and the distinction is the whole difference between single-stage and staged
group commit.

⇒ ✅ THE PREMISE HELD. Had it not, that would have been the result and the tables above
  would have been withdrawn rather than reported.
