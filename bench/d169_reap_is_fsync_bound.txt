=== 1. THE PRE-REGISTERED PREDICTION IS FALSIFIED ===================================

Registered: "the ON/OFF ratio must GROW ~LINEARLY in N — doubling the branch count roughly
doubles it", derived from `free_arena -> persist_if_configured -> persist_full_locked` being a
FULL rewrite once per branch reaped, giving `Σ 48·i = 24·N²`.

     N   OFF mean   ON mean   ratio
   100     1475.8    2031.0    1.38
   200     2915.3    4377.8    1.50
   400     6977.5    9009.4    1.29
   800    11611.1   19400.2    1.67
   (reap ms, mean of two counterbalanced blocks; `reaped` == N in every arm)

  log-log slope  OFF   : 1.019   (1.0 = linear, 2.0 = quadratic)
  log-log slope  ON    : 1.081
  log-log slope  RATIO : 0.062   ⇐ registered prediction was ~1.0

⇒ ⛔ **PREDICTION FALSIFIED. Outcome 3 (sub-linear), not outcome 1.** Both arms are linear in N.
An 8× increase in N moved the ratio 1.38 → 1.67, a factor of 1.21, not 8.

=== 2. OUTCOME 2 REQUIRED ME TO NAME THE BOUND. HERE IT IS. =========================

The prereg said: "Ratio FLAT in N ⇒ risk NOT realised. Then something bounds it that I have not
found, and **finding what is the result** — do not report 'no effect' without naming the bound."

**The bound is `src/branch/arena.rs:2378`, and it is D81 itself.** My prereg read D81's comment at
the CLAIM site ("the other three `persist_if_configured` sites keep the full rewrite deliberately")
and concluded the reaper still paid a full rewrite. It does not. Verbatim, at the FREE site:

  "**D81:** a 16-byte record and one fsync, not the whole image and two. The reaper's fast path is
   as frequent as the claim it mirrors in any workload that reaps what it forks, so leaving it on
   the full rewrite would cap this row's benefit at half."

`free_arena`'s fast path appends `TAIL_EXTENT_FREED` via `persist_delta_locked` — a delta, exactly
like the claim path. **So D81 fixed BOTH paths, the workload it names as its own risk is already
covered on the fast path, and the flat ratio is D81 working as designed, measured.** The three
surviving full-rewrite sites are the SLOW path and the pending-free log, not the per-branch free.

⇒ This is a CONFIRMATION of D81 on a second path, and a correction to my own prereg's premise.

=== 3. THE REAL WALL, AND IT IS AN INTEGER ==========================================

Durations are load-dependent; an operation count is not. Instrumenting `syncs_issued()` around
the sweep:

       N   reap ms   catalog syncs   syncs/branch
     100    1416.0            400          4.00
     200    2638.4            800          4.00
     400    5390.5           1600          4.00

⇒ ⭐ **EXACTLY 4.00 CATALOG FSYNCS PER BRANCH REAPED, at every N.** At ~13.5 ms/branch that is
~3.4 ms per fsync — the reap sweep is **fsync-bound**, not CPU- or map-bound.

**The phase table CLOSES — all four attributed, no remainder:**

  1. `reaper.rs:656`        `set_state(branch, Live -> Reaping)`
  2. `reaper.rs:275`        `detach_child(parent.id, fork_epoch)`
  3. `reaper.rs:713`        `set_state(branch, Reaping -> Reaped)`
  4. `table_catalog.rs:1332` `release_id(id)` — recycles the reaped id slot
     (confirmed on the reap path by `runtime.rs:725`: "catalog recycles a reaped branch's id SLOT
     (`reaper.rs` `release_id` ...)"; its body ends `stage()` / drop lock / `durable(seq)`)

=== 4. WHY GROUP COMMIT DOES NOT SAVE THIS — the mechanism, read from source ========

Every one of the four is *built* to batch. `table_catalog.rs:1373`, on `detach_child`:

  "Lock dropped BEFORE the fsync, so this joins the commit group rather than ..."

**But `reap_expired` is a SERIAL loop** — `reaper.rs:733`, `for rec in candidates`, no threads, no
rayon, no chunked parallelism. Group commit batches *concurrent* committers. A single-threaded
sweep supplies none, so each of the four `durable(seq)` calls is its own fsync.

⇒ **SHAPE, and it transfers to any engine:** *the mechanism that gave `fork` ×17.3 (f/sync
1.00 → 17.12) is present on the reap path and structurally cannot fire there, because the sweep
that would feed it is serial.* The batching primitive and the workload that needs it are in the
same binary and never meet.

⇒ **ARITHMETIC, NOT A MEASUREMENT, and labelled as such:** 10⁶ branches × 4 fsyncs × ~3.4 ms
≈ 3.8 hours of pure durability latency on reap alone, if the sweep stays serial and fsync cost
stays flat. Neither has been measured at that scale here. Largest N measured: 800.
