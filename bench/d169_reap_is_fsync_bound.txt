=== 1. THE PRE-REGISTERED PREDICTION IS FALSIFIED ===================================

Registered: "the ON/OFF ratio must GROW ~LINEARLY in N — doubling the branch count roughly
doubles it", derived from `free_arena -> persist_if_configured -> persist_full_locked` being a
FULL rewrite once per branch reaped, giving `Σ 48·i = 24·N²`.

     N   OFF mean   ON mean   ratio
   100     1475.8    2031.0    1.38
   200     2915.3    4377.8    1.50
   400     6977.5    9009.4    1.29
   800    11611.1   19400.2    1.67
   (reap ms, mean of two counterbalanced blocks; `reaped` == N in every arm)

  log-log slope  OFF   : 1.019   (1.0 = linear, 2.0 = quadratic)
  log-log slope  ON    : 1.081
  log-log slope  RATIO : 0.062   ⇐ registered prediction was ~1.0

⇒ ⛔ **PREDICTION FALSIFIED. Outcome 3 (sub-linear), not outcome 1.** Both arms are linear in N.
An 8× increase in N moved the ratio 1.38 → 1.67, a factor of 1.21, not 8.

=== 3. THE WALL IS FSYNCS, AND THE COUNT IS A FUNCTION OF TREE SHAPE ================

⛔ **THIS SECTION WAS REWRITTEN 2026-09-23 AFTER ADVERSARIAL REVIEW** (`frontier/reap_fsync_adversary.md`,
verdict SURVIVES-NARROWED). What it said first — "**EXACTLY 4.00 CATALOG FSYNCS PER BRANCH REAPED,
at every N**" — is **true of the axis I varied and false of the axis I did not**. Kept visible here
rather than quietly replaced.

Instrumenting `syncs_issued()` around the sweep, in `d31_reap_cost`'s fixture:

       N   reap ms   catalog syncs   syncs/branch
     100    1416.0            400          4.00
     200    2638.4            800          4.00
     400    5390.5           1600          4.00

⚠ **But `d31_reap_cost.rs:87` forks EVERY branch from `BranchId::TRUNK` with a 1 ms lease**, so the
sweep sees N childless leaves whose parent is Live. **4 is the cost of THAT SHAPE, not of a reap.**
The adversary measured one `reap` at a time across three shapes:

  ARM A  star, 6 leaves of a LIVE trunk (= this fixture)      4 each      ⇒ 4.00/branch
  ARM B  chain depth 8, DEEPEST-FIRST (expired order)         4 each      ⇒ 4.00/branch
  ARM C  chain depth 8, SHALLOWEST-FIRST                      2 each, then 11  ⇒ 3.12/branch

⇒ ✅ **THE TRUE STATEMENT:  2 ≤ syncs ≤ 3 + (reaped-ancestor chain length)**, with 4 the value for
a childless leaf whose parent is not itself `Reaped`. Arm C is production-reachable:
`expired_candidates` sorts deepest-first only among branches **already** expired
(`reaper.rs:795-806`), and an ancestor's lease can expire first — then `detach_from_parent` returns
early at `reaper.rs:268`, `release_id` refuses, the reap costs 2, the id slot is never recycled, and
the cost lands multiplied on whichever descendant is reaped last. ⚠ The TOTAL moves opposite to
intuition: the same 8-chain costs **32** fsyncs deepest-first and **25** shallowest-first.

**The four sites, ATTRIBUTION CONFIRMED BY MEASUREMENT, not by reading.** Scope enumerated from the
type (13 `durable()` sites across 12 methods in `table_catalog.rs`); `reap` reaches only these four,
everything else it calls is a read or an atomic (`next_epoch` is a `fetch_add`, the obvious fifth
candidate, and it is clean). `free_arena` ruled out by varying extents/branch **15×** (1.00 → 15.00)
with syncs/branch flat at 4.00 throughout.

  1. `reaper.rs:656`         `set_state(branch, Live -> Reaping)`
  2. `reaper.rs:713`         `set_state(branch, Reaping -> Reaped)`
  3. `reaper.rs:714`         `detach_from_parent` -> `detach_child`, **once per already-Reaped ancestor**
  4. `table_catalog.rs:1332` `release_id(id)` — only `if reusable`, i.e. no live children

⛔ **The first version of this table printed the order 1, detach, set_state, release_id — which is
BACKWARDS, and is the ordering the source names as a historical CORRECTNESS BUG.** `reaper.rs:690`,
in capitals: *"MARK REAPED FIRST, THEN DETACH. The reverse of what this used to do, and the order is
load-bearing rather than cosmetic … Detaching first would let a crash leave NO entry for a child that
still reads Live."* It changed no count and would have misled anyone using the table as a map.

=== 4. WHY THE FSYNCS DO NOT BATCH — and my first answer cited the wrong loop ========

⛔ **RETRACTED: §4 originally cited `reap_expired` (`reaper.rs:733`, `for rec in candidates`).
`reap_expired` HAS NO PRODUCTION CALLER.** Every occurrence in `src/` is the trait declaration, the
definition, a doc comment, or below `reaper.rs:881` which is `#[cfg(test)]`; the only non-test callers
are three `examples/`. `lease_thread.rs:102` says it outright: *"the trait's `reap_expired` is the
whole-sweep shape"*. **I cited the harness while making a claim about the engine** — precisely what
this file's own closing bullet warns against.

✅ **The conclusion holds anyway, and was UNDERSTATED.** Production sweeps via
`lease_thread::scan_once`, which has its own serial loop (`lease_thread.rs:630-636`,
`candidates.chunks(REAP_CHUNK)` with `REAP_CHUNK == 1`) — and each reap runs inside
`with_lock(...)` (`lease_thread.rs:634`) where that lock is the pgwire **per-statement mutex**
(`impl RuntimeLock for pgwire::ServerContext`, `lease_thread.rs:214`). `reaper.rs:535` states it from
the other side: *"`reap_expired` runs under the per-statement lock every `fork` also takes."*

⇒ **SHAPE, and it transfers:** *while a reap issues its fsyncs, no other statement in the database
can even stage a ticket.* Group commit batches concurrent committers; this path excludes them by
construction. **Parallelising the sweep would not fix it — removing the statement lock (W4) would.**

⇒ ⛔ **AND THE FIX I NAMED WAS THE WRONG PRIMITIVE.** Group commit is not what collapses these.
The primitive that does is **`stage()` without `durable()`**, which this catalog ALREADY HAS as
D159's `fork_staged` / `await_fork_durable(seq)` (`table_catalog.rs:913`), because a later `durable()`
covers an earlier staged write — `flush_all` takes no `seq` (`table_catalog.rs:352-372`). **A reap
could stage all four and sync once per sweep, with no concurrency at all.**

=== 4b. THE PER-FSYNC COST — independently measured, and it got STRONGER ============

§3's original "~3.4 ms per fsync" was a load-dependent duration divided by a clean integer, i.e.
unfalsifiable as written. Measured directly instead, same filesystem, same call `DiskManager::sync`
makes (`self.storage.sync_all()`, `disk_manager.rs:487-489` — `fcntl(F_FULLFSYNC)` on macOS):

  sync_all,  0 dirty pages   n=200  mean 0.000 ms   p50 0.000   max 0.003
  sync_all,  1 dirty 4K page n=200  mean **3.035 ms**  p50 2.999   max 4.376
  sync_all,  8 dirty pages   n=200  mean 3.067 ms   p50 2.994   max 5.587
  sync_all, 64 dirty pages   n=100  mean 3.022 ms   p50 2.914   max 6.412

⇒ **~3.0 ms per fsync, flat in dirty-page count** — it is the barrier, not the bytes. So a 4-fsync
reap has a **~12 ms floor** that is a property of the disk, not of ferrodb, and no amount of CPU work
removes it.

⚠ **THE FLOOR IS LOAD-IMMUNE; THE FRACTION IS NOT. State this row in the INTEGER, not the
millisecond.** 4 × 3.03 = 12.1 ms is ~90% of the 13.5 ms/branch measured on a quiet box — but the
adversary's own `d31` run at load 20 showed **the same 4.00 syncs and 22.3 ms/branch**, where 12.1 ms
is only **54%**. Its durations were not even monotone in work (1113.7 ms at 1 extent/branch vs
766.6 ms at 15). ⇒ The **12 ms floor** and the **integer 4** survive any load; **"fsync is ~90% of
reap" does not** and must not be quoted without the load it was taken at.

- ⛔ **THE NOVELTY CLAIM WAS TOO BROAD AND IS NARROWED.** What is new here is the **sync COUNT and
  its ATTRIBUTION**, not the characterisation. **"Reap is fsync-bound" is already asserted in this

    • `bench/land-2655e3b_certified/SUMMARY.txt:28` — *"This box is fsync-bound — a reap costs

  ⭐ **What those three do NOT say is the number.** All three model a reap as *"**a** durable

  ⚠ Their ~58 ms and 16–38 ms vs this row's 13.5 ms are different boxes, loads and shapes; the
