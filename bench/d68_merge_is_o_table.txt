**MERGE IS O(TABLE). CONFIRMED, AND CONFIRMED BY PREDICTION RATHER THAN BY FITTING.**

  rows     median ms   ms/1000 rows   time for a doubling

Two independent readings of the same curve, both saying linear:
  * ms-per-1000-rows CONVERGES to 2.00 and stops falling. A flat ms-per-unit column IS linearity;
    the early fall is a fixed cost being amortised, not sublinear growth.
  * The cost of a DOUBLING converges to 2.00x: 1.19 -> 1.48 -> 1.61 -> 1.88. That is the same
    statement from the other side.

FIT, and the reason it is not curve-fitting: on the 1k and 4k points alone this session fitted
t = 4.5 ms + 1.52 us/row and PREDICTED 16.7 ms at 8,000 rows BEFORE that point was measured. It
measured 17.050 — 2% out. Refitting on the tail (8k, 16k) gives the asymptote:

    t  ~=  2.1 ms  +  1.87 us/row

WHAT THAT COEFFICIENT MEANS AT THE OBJECTIVE'S SCALE, by arithmetic and labelled as such:
    100,000-row table  ->  ~190 ms per merge
  1,000,000-row table  ->  ~1.9 SECONDS per merge
...to write FOUR ROWS, and while holding the global catalog mutex the whole time.

THE MECHANISM, verified in source before this run, not inferred from it:
  * runtime.rs:2691, in `evaluate_merge`  — `for row in scan_table(name, &ctx.read())?`
  * runtime.rs:3459, in `fingerprint_tables` (from `publish_evaluation_as`) — the SAME scan again
  * runtime.rs:4317, `fingerprint_rows` — hashes EVERY ROW to build `base_fingerprint`
  A merge reads the whole table TWICE and hashes it once, regardless of how little it changed.
  1.87 us/row over two scans plus a hash is the right order for that work.

⚠ WHY NOBODY SAW IT: at 1,000 rows the scan is a quarter of the merge and reads as overhead; the
fixed ~2-4 ms hides it. Only the SIZE AXIS separates a constant from a class
([[measure-the-slope-not-the-ratio]]), and every prior ferrodb fixture is small.

⚠ ABSOLUTES ARE INFLATED — load was 74-80 throughout, from other sessions. That scales all five

SLOPE is quoted and the milliseconds are not.

⚠ WHAT THIS IS NOT: not a BranchBench result, not a comparison against Dolt or Postgres. It is a
SHAPE — "merge cost is independent of how much the branch changed" — and it transfers to any
engine that validates a merge by fingerprinting the target rather than the delta.
