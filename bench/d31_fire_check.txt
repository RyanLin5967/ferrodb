D31 — fire-check of the growth rule and of the verdict classifier.

WHICH DIRECTION IS THE PASS, stated first because it is the thing most easily read backwards:

  A fire-check PASSES when breaking the mechanism CHANGES the number and flips the verdict.
  It FAILS if the mutant produces the same reading as the fixed tree — that would mean the
  measurement is not sensitive to the thing it claims to measure, and the green result would be
  telling us nothing. "The mutant made it fail" is the GOOD outcome here, not a defect.

Instrument: examples/branch_curve_writes.rs, N = 496 branches x 1 page written, 8 threads,
release build, rebuilt per mutant. Space columns only; see the note on wall-clock below.

1. THE FIXED TREE (HEAD, no mutation)
     4104 len B/branch
     VERDICT — AMPLIFICATION GONE.

2. MUTANT A — `next_extent_pages` returns ARENA_EXTENT_PAGES unconditionally, i.e. the flat
   pre-D31 allocator. This is the mutation that says whether geometric growth is doing the work.
     1046478 len B/branch
     VERDICT — AMPLIFICATION PRESENT.
   PASS: 4104 -> 1046478, a 255x change, and the verdict flips. The number is the pre-D31 figure
   to the byte (bench/d31_before.txt reports 1046478 at this same N), so the mutant reproduces
   the original wall exactly rather than merely moving in the right direction.

3. MUTANT B — `ARENA_FIRST_EXTENT_PAGES = 16`, chosen to land BETWEEN the two reference values
   so the classifier's third branch is forced rather than assumed.
     65420 len B/branch
     VERDICT — AMBIGUOUS. ... "Do NOT read this as either result."
   PASS: the classifier refuses to classify instead of rounding to the nearer reference. A
   detector that never fires its own "I cannot tell" branch has not been shown to have one.

So all three branches of `verdict()` were made to fire on purpose, and the growth rule was shown
to be load-bearing rather than incidental.
