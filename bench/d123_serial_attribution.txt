D8's result, quoted into INVENTION-TRIGGER.md as what established W-B: "throughput = 1/serial_time
predicted 4,386 against 4,382.7 measured — 0.15% agreement across four runs."

That is division. In fork_concurrency.rs the two printed columns are

    per-fork ms = secs*1000/total          forks/sec = total/secs

which are algebraic reciprocals, so "1/per-fork-ms agrees with forks/sec" is true of ANY numbers
the harness could ever print. Checked against this row's own runs, where the same two columns are
computed the same way:

    T=1    forks/sec  244.7   1000/S_eff  244.7   ratio 0.999878
    T=8    forks/sec 1165.8   1000/S_eff 1165.8   ratio 0.999965
    T=64   forks/sec 5057.3   1000/S_eff 5057.1   ratio 1.000031
    T=512  forks/sec 4942.7   1000/S_eff 4942.7   ratio 1.000007

And the inherited residual is reproduced exactly as a ROUNDING artifact:

    measured 4382.7/sec -> exact per-fork 0.228170 ms -> PRINTED to 3 dp as 0.228
    1 / 0.228 ms = 4385.96/sec         discrepancy vs measured  +0.074%
                                       discrepancy from rounding +0.074%   <- identical

⇒ The 0.15% was the third decimal place of a rounded reciprocal. **It is not evidence that the

2a. F4 — HOLD vs HANDOFF. DECIDED BY ONE NUMBER, MEASURED FOUR TIMES.

HOLD_TOTAL is stamped to the UNLOCK, not to the end of the work: the guard is declared first so it
drops LAST, and the record drops before it are lock-held too (Amendment 1 — without bracketing
those, allocator work would have been misreported as handoff).

runs) gives 0.75x and 0.66x — which agrees with (A). The 1.42x is the regime error of section 2d
wearing a ratio's clothing, in a column of my own harness. The label is the bug, not the slope.

⚠ **THIS MATTERS BEYOND BOOKKEEPING: 1.42x was used to kill a Round 3 candidate (flat combining).**
The number points the OPPOSITE way once same-instrument: adding work to the critical section costs
LESS per unit under contention, not more. That kill should be re-examined — though NOT simply
reversed, because flat combining would batch REAL fork work, which is NEW-key insertion, and
new-key work is the one thing here that does inflate (2.29x / 2.43x). The right multiplier for
flat combining is the new-key one, which is what the novelty arm (section 3c) measures. Neither
0.74x nor 1.42x is the number that decides it.

================================================================================================
3. NAMING THE MECHANISM — WHAT SURVIVES ELIMINATION
================================================================================================

The brief listed five candidates. Four are eliminated by measurement or by structure.

⛔ MUTEX HANDOFF / PARK-UNPARK. Eliminated by F4: the lock is idle 0.006-0.012 ms per fork, 3-6%
   of the interval (U(hold) 94-97%). The unlock itself is separately bracketed at 0.002 ms and the
   record drops at 0.00002 ms. This was the mechanism the brief thought most likely and it is the
   smallest term measured.

⛔ BUFFER-POOL EVICTION. Eliminated FREE, from the two regimes already banked. The pool is capped
   at 1024 pages (buffer_pool.rs:442). The warm=20,000 tree (~100k keys) strains it; the warm=0
   tree does not. Eviction predicts the LARGE tree inflates more. It inflates LESS:
       64/1          warm=20k (large)   warm=0 (small)
       write_record       2.29x            2.36x
       child_key          2.43x            2.61x
       HOLD               1.61x            1.71x
   Wrong direction in both phases and in the total. No extra arm was needed and none was run.

⛔ PAGE-LATCH CONTENTION BETWEEN FORKERS, ALLOCATOR CONTENTION BETWEEN FORKERS, CACHE-LINE
   BOUNCING BETWEEN FORKERS. Eliminated STRUCTURALLY, not statistically: `fork` does every tree
   mutation under `logical`, so **only one thread is ever inside the section**.

   CHECKED AGAINST THE CODE THAT RAN, not from memory. Enumerating every tree access in `fork`
   by line offset within the function (src/branch/table_catalog.rs):
       +14  let g = self.logical.lock()            <- acquired
       +26  self.core(parent.id)                   \
       +38  self.envelope_bytes(parent.id)          |
       +50  self.tree.range_scan(FREE_ID span)      |
       +64  self.remove_if_present(free_id)         | EVERY tree access in the function
       +65  self.core(id)                           | lies strictly between the two
       +86  self.write_record  / +88 _new           |
      +100  self.tree.insert(child key)             |
      +115  self.upsert (F5 extras)                 |
      +124  self.write_header()                     |
      +129  self.stage()                           /
      +141  drop(g)                                <- released
      +148  self.durable(seq)                      <- THE ONLY THING OUTSIDE
   There is no tree access before +14 or between +141 and +148. So the set of things that can run
   concurrently with the section is exactly {`durable`}, and `durable` is
   `pool.flush_all()` + `disk_manager.sync()`. Two forkers cannot
   contend on a page latch, an allocator or a cache line because two forkers are never in there at
   once. Combined with U(hold) = 94-97%, which puts the time INSIDE the section, the counterparty
   must be something that runs OUTSIDE the lock — and in this harness exactly one thing does:
   `durable()` -> `pool.flush_all()` + `disk_manager.sync()`, the group-commit flush.

⛔ CACHE-LINE BOUNCING ON SHARED TREE PAGES specifically. Eliminated by F5, and this was
   pre-registered in Amendment 3 as the reading the fixed-key design would license: the extra
   upserts hit the SAME few pages from all 64 threads, which is the best case for residency and
   the WORST case for line bouncing. If lines were bouncing, that arm would inflate most. It is
   the CHEAPEST thing measured:
       identical fixed-key upsert work:  T=1 0.02994 ms/upsert  ->  T=64 0.02038 ms/upsert = 0.68x

✅ SURVIVING, NAMED: INTERFERENCE BETWEEN THE SECTION'S NEW-PAGE WRITES AND THE CONCURRENT
   GROUP-COMMIT FLUSH. Four counters select it and they all point the same way:
     1. only the two phases that insert NEW keys inflate      (2.29x, 2.43x)
     2. reads and the single hot header page get FASTER       (0.43-0.92x)
     3. identical work on ALREADY-DIRTY pages gets faster     (0.68x)
     4. inflation tracks how many forks each flush must drain:
            f/sync     1.0    4.0   17.5
            write_record 1.00x 1.59x 2.29x     monotone
   At T=64, DURABLE is 5.35 ms against S_eff 0.19 ms, so a flush is in progress essentially all the
   time; at T=1 the section and the flush strictly alternate and never overlap. The inflation
   appears as soon as they can overlap (T=8, 1.27x) and grows with how much they overlap.

⚠ WHAT IS STILL NOT SEPARATED, STATED PLAINLY PER F3. "Interference with the flush" covers both a
  latch/pool path and a file-extension/IO path, and this row does not separate them. Nor has an arm
  been run with the flush REMOVED, because removing it removes durability and macOS then
  short-circuits F_FULLFSYNC — the confound guarded against throughout. The mechanism is named by
  elimination plus four selectivity counters plus a structural argument, NOT by direct
  intervention on the flush itself.

3c. ⭐ THE INTERVENTION — NEW-KEY vs FIXED-KEY WORK UNDER THE LOCK. MECHANISM NAMED.
================================================================================================

in one arm they hit a FIXED key set (re-dirtying pages already dirty, allocating nothing), in the

in the section. **Key novelty is the only variable.**

⇒ Combined with the structural fact that **only one thread is ever inside `logical`** (section 3,
enumerated line by line), the counterparty cannot be another forker. It is `durable()` —
`pool.flush_all()` + `disk_manager.sync()` — the group-commit flush draining the pages the section
keeps creating. That is the answer to Q1's "name it with a counter, not a guess."

⚠ THE CONFOUND, TESTED RATHER THAN ASSUMED. A reviewer raised that new-key inflation might be
TREE GROWTH: new keys are the only component that enlarges the tree, so if higher T completed more
forks per window it would grow the tree more and deepen every descent. That objection requires a
TIME-bounded run. This harness is FORK-bounded: `run_arm` does `let per = n / t.max(1)`, so N=8,000
is the TOTAL divided across threads. The same 8,000 forks insert the same 64,000 keys at T=1 and at
T=64 — identical final tree, identical growth trajectory, present in both numerator and denominator
of `R_new`. **It cancels, as Amendment 9 pre-registered before the run.**

⚠ And the absolute ms/upsert is higher for NEW at BOTH thread counts, for that same reason. That
difference is the tree, not contention. Only the ratios are quoted.

================================================================================================
3b. ⭐ OF WHAT IS INSIDE THE SECTION, HOW MUCH IS THE FIVE DURABLE KEYS?  88%.
    (Asked by the lead after round 3 closed on "multi-key atomicity over five durable keys".)
================================================================================================

The five keys are: `write_record` = 3 (record, state, deadline), `child_key_insert` = 1, and
`write_header` = 1. Split of HOLD at T=64, three independent runs:

                                  warm=20k        warm=0     re-run rep1
  FIVE DURABLE KEYS                 88.4%          88.4%         88.0%
    of which 4 NEW keys             77.6%          76.4%            -     <- the ONLY inflating part
    of which 1 hot key (header)     10.8%          12.0%            -     <- gets FASTER with T
  everything else                   11.5%          11.5%            -     2 point reads, 1 range
                                                                          scan, id mint, ticket
  cost per NEW key at T=64        0.03686 ms     0.03800 ms

⇒ **The section essentially IS the five keys.** Reads, the id mint and the commit ticket together
are 11.5%, so no amount of cleverness about them matters. Whatever replaces this section has to
replace the key writes.
⇒ **But the four NEW keys are the whole problem, and the fifth is not.** `write_header` rewrites
one already-hot page every fork and gets CHEAPER under concurrency (0.65-0.92x). A mechanism that
reduced five keys to four by folding the header away would remove the one key that costs nothing
extra under load.

### ⛔ AND THE RETURN ON REMOVING KEYS SATURATES — THIS IS THE NUMBER ROUND 3 NEEDS MOST
From the warm=0 stub table at T=64:

   stub  forks/sec     S_eff  HOLD_TOT       gap  U(hold)
      0     4788.6   0.20883   0.20079   0.00804    96.1%
      1     8447.6   0.11838   0.07707   0.04131    65.1%
      2     8988.9   0.11125   0.07188   0.03937    64.6%
      3     9019.1   0.11088   0.00941   0.10147     8.5%

HOLD falls **21x** (0.20079 -> 0.00941). Throughput rises only **1.9x**, because the GAP rises
**12.6x** (0.00804 -> 0.10147) and lock utilisation collapses 96.1% -> 8.5%.

⇒ **THE SECTION AND THE HANDOFF TRADE OFF AGAINST EACH OTHER.** A critical section that short
cannot be handed between 64 parked threads fast enough to keep the lock busy, so park/unpark
becomes the binding term. This is why F1 measures ~2x and not the ~21x that `throughput = 1/S`
would predict from the same numbers — **and it is a second, independent demonstration that
`throughput = 1/serial_time` is not a law of this system.** It held in the L0 regime because the
lock was 96% busy there; it fails by an order of magnitude as soon as the section is short.

⇒ So the answer to "fewer keys, or a different atomicity mechanism" is: **fewer keys buys at most
~2x and the curve is already flat by L1** (8447 -> 9019 from removing three more keys' worth of
work, i.e. 6.8% for the last two levels). A different atomicity mechanism is the only thing in
this data with more than 2x in it, and it has to remove the SERIALISATION, not the work — because
the work is already only 88% of a term that stops binding once you shrink it.

4. VERDICT ON W-B, AND WHAT CHANGES FOR ROUND 3
================================================================================================

⭐ **W-B IS CORRECTLY SPECIFIED. THE EVIDENCE IT WAS SPECIFIED ON WAS NOT.**

The claim "the serial section is the ceiling" is TRUE and is now backed by two measurements that
could have come out the other way:
  * F1 — driving the section toward zero moves throughput by a paired median of 2.00x across 18
    draws in two regimes (warm=0 2.08x, warm=20k 1.86x), with 17 of 18 individual draws above
    1.10x against a falsifier that required 1.00x +/-10%.
  * F6 — M-FSYNC refuted on a pure counter: f/sync stalls at ~17 while T grows to 512.

The evidence it was ORIGINALLY specified on — D8's "throughput = 1/serial_time, 0.15% agreement
across four runs" — is an algebraic identity between two columns that are reciprocals by
construction, and its 0.15% residual is exactly the rounding of 0.228170 to 0.228. It could not
have failed, so it never discriminated anything. **W-B was right by accident.**

The adversary's kill was ALSO wrong, and wrong in a way arithmetic alone disposes of: 64 forks per
3.574 ms is 17,907/sec, four times ABOVE the 4,382/sec it was invoked to explain.

### So both of Round 2's closing statements about W-B were wrong, in opposite directions.
INVENTION-TRIGGER.md:148 currently reads "⛔ MIS-SPECIFIED ... 67.2% unattributed ... fsync-bound
at 64 forks per 3.574 ms round, so even a serial section driven to ZERO cannot clear the bar."
Every clause of that needs replacing:
  * "67.2% unattributed"  -> 78.8% attributed; and roughly half of the 0.1532 ms was never a
    physical cost, it was four regimes subtracted from each other.
  * "fsync-bound"          -> refuted on a counter, in both regimes, at six thread counts.
  * "even a serial section driven to ZERO cannot clear the bar" -> driven to its limit it gives
    about 2x (paired median 2.00x). Which is the real constraint on Round 3, and it is a budget,
    not a wall.

### WHAT ROUND 3 SHOULD BE TOLD, AND IT IS A DIFFERENT SHAPE OF TARGET
1. **The budget for serial-section work is ~2x, measured.** L3 is the limit of that strategy and it
   is not implementable. A candidate promising more than 2x from shortening fork's critical section
   is promising more than the section contains.
2. **The two phases worth attacking are the two that insert NEW KEYS** — `write_record` and the
   child-key insert. They are 76% of the section at 64 threads and they are the ONLY parts that get
   worse with concurrency. Everything else gets faster.
3. **Handoff is not worth attacking.** The lock is idle 3-6% of the interval. A better mutex, a
   lock-free queue or elision buys at most that.
4. **⭐ THE LARGER PRIZE IS OUTSIDE THE SECTION ENTIRELY, and this row can see it but did not
   measure it as a candidate.** At T=64 a thread spends 0.19 ms in the section and 12.2 ms waiting
   (WAIT 6.84 + DURABLE 5.35). The section is the CEILING ON THROUGHPUT because it serialises, but
   it is 1.5% of LATENCY. A mechanism that lets forks commit without every one of them passing
   through one mutex — sharding the catalog by parent, or batching fork admission the way group
   commit already batches fsync — changes the shape rather than the constant. D6's own note already
   points at this ("option 4, pre-minting ids in durable blocks"), and it is the only direction in
   this row's data with more than 2x in it.

6. INSTRUMENT, LOAD, AND WHAT IS NOT TRUSTWORTHY HERE
================================================================================================

* Probe perturbation MEASURED, not assumed: -1.20% (warm=20k) and -1.83% (warm=0), 5 reps each,
  ON/OFF order alternated. Inside the pre-registered +/-2%. The first battery's single-shot
  +87.95% does not reproduce; that cell was n=1 and its OFF value (2796.6) was the outlier, not
  its ON value — the reading had inverted which cell was anomalous.
* The probe adds ~13 clock reads inside the section. That is common-mode between T=1 and T=64, so
  it DILUTES the 64/1 ratios toward 1.0. The inflation figures are therefore understated, not
  manufactured.
* `Instant::elapsed` quantises to 41.67 ns here. Every figure is a sum over >=8,000 samples
  (>=4,500 ticks for a 0.19 ms section). No single-operation duration is reported anywhere.
* LOAD. Lock held for every timed run. `load_at_acquire=4` for the re-run; no CAVEAT line printed
  (threshold is 8). But load RAMPED to 11.05 during 10_f1_warm20k and that arm is the one that came
  out noise-dominated. The measure lock excludes other MEASURERS, not builds or suites, so "lock
  held" is not "box quiet" and is not claimed to be. EVERY duration here is an UPPER BOUND.
* ⭐ WHAT IS NOT TRUSTWORTHY: any absolute millisecond figure, and the warm=20k F1 arm. What IS
  trustworthy: the counters (f/sync, syncs x f/sync = N), the COUNT of paired F1 draws above
  1.10x (17 of 18),
  and ratios of two durations taken by ONE instrument inside ONE process (U(hold), the 64/1
  column, per-upsert cost at fixed k) — which is where every conclusion above rests.
* A tree-size confound exists in F1 and is bounded rather than removed: stubbed levels insert fewer
  keys, so their trees grow more slowly inside the timed window. The depth-sensitive phases
  (core + envelope + free_scan) total ~0.022 ms of a ~0.19 ms section, so even a 20% swing in them
  moves F1 by ~2%, against an effect of 105%.
