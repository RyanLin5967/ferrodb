Read it this way: if forks/sec is FLAT across threads, the forks are serializing and
group commit has nothing to batch. That is the D6 premise check, as a number.

BEFORE, same harness, same arms (bench/fork_concurrency_before.txt):
  1 thread 274.9/sec | 8 threads 295.8/sec | 64 threads 254.0/sec   -- FLAT, x0.92 at 64

INTERPRETATION, written after the numbers:

  Group commit works: 254 -> 4382 forks/sec at 64 threads, x17.3. 10^6 branches goes from ~62
  minutes of durability latency to ~3.8 minutes.

  BUT THE TARGET WAS 10k/sec AND IT PLATEAUS AT ~4,400. The bottleneck MOVED, and forks/fsync is
  what names where to. It rises 1.0 -> 3.9 -> 13.8 and then STOPS: 64 and 256 threads both give
  13.8, and both give ~4,380 forks/sec. A batch that stops growing while the thread count
  quadruples is not a disk limit.

  The arithmetic identifies it exactly:
    per-fork wall at 64 threads          0.228 ms
    1 / 0.228 ms                       = 4386 forks/sec   vs 4382.7 MEASURED
    one fsync                            3.574 ms  (the 1-thread per-fork cost, which is 1 fsync)
    3.574 / 0.228                      = 15.7 forks able to pass the lock during one fsync
                                              vs 13.8 MEASURED forks per fsync
  So the batch size is not set by the disk at all -- it is set by HOW MANY FORKS CAN GET THROUGH
  `logical` WHILE ONE FSYNC RUNS. Throughput equals the rate of the serial section. The wall is
  now the tree mutations under the logical mutex, at ~0.23 ms each.

  That is D6a's falsifier firing in the direction it named: "if forks still serialize after the
  split, the contention is deeper -- name the next lock before believing the first fix." Named:
  `logical`, and the cost is the ~6 tree operations each fork performs while holding it.

  The 1-thread arm got SLIGHTLY SLOWER (274.9 -> 279.8 here, and 245.9 in an earlier run; call it
  unchanged-to-slightly-worse). A lone forker has nobody to share an fsync with and pays the
  condvar bookkeeping for nothing. That is the honest cost of this change and it is exactly why D6
  keeps option 4 (pre-minting ids in durable blocks, i.e. Oracle/Postgres sequence caching) on the
  table: it is the SERIAL forker's answer, and the two are complementary, not alternatives.
