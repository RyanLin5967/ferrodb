=== THE OBJECTIVE, REACHED. 10^6 branches in one catalog. ===
Largest N previously measured in this project: 64,000. This is 15.6x beyond it.
Predictions were committed BEFORE the run (artie-research/S12-PREDICTIONS.md, 8136194).

SCORING THOSE PREDICTIONS HONESTLY:

  ✅ CONFIRMED -- the predicted NON-knee. "The buffer pool does NOT thrash despite being 60x
     oversubscribed, because every insert on the fork path lands at the EDGE of its span."
     4.2 MB pool against a 266 MB catalog = 63x, and throughput did not collapse. The stated
     signature of the failure (forks/sec collapsing with forks/fsync still ~14, i.e. a disk busy
     with READS) never appeared.

  ❌ WRONG -- the predicted knee. "The B+tree gains a level (log300(1e6)=2.42 -> 3), so expect a
     20-35% drop to ~2900-3500 forks/sec." Throughput is FLAT TO RISING across 100x: 2901.6 at 10k
     and 3778.8 in the final segment. Either the depth model is wrong, or an extra hop is lost in
     noise against the rest of the serial section. Recorded as wrong; not explained, because an
     explanation invented after the fact is not a finding.

  ⚠ INSTRUMENT ERROR, caught mid-run and corrected before the last rows: "reopen flat ~10us" was
     derived from a measurement of open(pool, root) while this harness times open_sidecar, which
     also builds a DiskManager and a 4 MB pool. The numbers are not comparable. WHAT THE RUN DOES
     SHOW is the claim that matters: reopen DOES NOT GROW WITH N -- 0.5977 ms at 10k, 0.2415 ms at
     10^6. O(1) holds to 10^6.

  ≈ CLOSE: catalog 250 MB predicted / 266.2 MB actual (+6.5%). marginal RSS 15.2 B/branch predicted
     / ~7.7 B/branch actual (better than predicted). forks/fsync ~14 predicted / 16-25 actual.

WHAT IS LINEAR, AS FLAGGED IN ADVANCE RATHER THAN TRIPPED OVER:
  live_count 0.664 -> 59.234 ms across 100x N. Exactly linear. At 10^6 it walks a million entries
  to produce one integer. It is not on the fork path, so it does not distort any number above --
  which is why it was timed separately and on purpose.

WHAT THIS IS EVIDENCE FOR, AND WHAT IT IS NOT:
  ferrodb is a proof of concept. The ABSOLUTE numbers here are worth little: a favourable one would
  be an artifact of what ferrodb does not implement. What transfers is the SHAPE, and the shapes are
  the point:
    - catalog space LINEAR in branches (266 B/branch constant to 4 significant figures over 100x)
    - reopen INDEPENDENT of branch count out to 10^6
    - fork throughput NOT degrading with branch count out to 10^6
  Proving that branch-per-agent survives a real engine -- an existing WAL, MVCC, recovery code
  nobody may break -- requires a fork of an established database and CANNOT be claimed from here.
