1. THE INSTRUMENT FAILED ITS OWN POSITIVE CONTROL FIRST, AND THE PREMISE WAS WHAT WAS WRONG
================================================================================================

Raw: bench/ceiling_raw/00_model_POSITIVE_CONTROL_FAILED.txt (exit 1, banked before anything was
touched).

The scaffold carried a gate asserting that 64 threads doing nothing but take and release one
`Mutex` must show contended/op near 1.0. First run:

  NEG single-thread  T=1   contended/op 0.00000   acq/op 1.0000   -> PASSED
  POS 64t all-lock   T=64  contended/op 0.04688   acq/op 1.0000   -> REFUSED against >= 0.5

`acq/op` was exactly 1.0000 and the negative control exactly 0.00000, so the bookkeeping was
sound and only the premise was not. **Mechanism, written into the commit before the replacement
was run:** `std::sync::Mutex` barges. Unlocking is a store plus at most a wake syscall; the woken
waiter needs a scheduler round trip, while the releasing thread's next `try_lock` CAS costs
nanoseconds. With no work outside the section the releaser re-wins its own lock, so a permanently
saturated mutex still reports `try_lock` SUCCESS.

⇒ ⛔ **`try_lock`-failure counts "the lock was held by another thread at the instant I tried". It
does NOT count "threads are blocked". THIS IS A BLIND SPOT IN AMENDMENT 2'S CHOSEN INSTRUMENT**,
and it is measured, not argued — bench/ceiling_raw/03_model_closed_form.txt:

  BARGE 64t no-out   contended/op 0.03828   queue_mean 55.91   queue_max 64

56 of 64 threads were inside the acquire region on average, and the census read 0.038. Against
the ~1.0 a saturated queue should produce, that is a 26x under-report; against the same file's
genuinely-queued POS cell (0.52994) it is 14x. **Direction of the error: contended/op
UNDER-reports blocking**, i.e. it errs toward killing outcome 1. Every low reading in this file
inherits that, which is why outcome 2 is NOT rested on the count alone — see section 5.

The queue gauge (`IN_ACQ`) that exposes this came from the paused session's uncommitted tree;
see "PROVENANCE" below.

5. ⭐ WHAT THE REMAINING TIME IS — THE NUMBER NO COLLISION COUNT COULD HAVE GIVEN
================================================================================================

Every thread's cycle is wait -> hold -> durable, and the probe measures all three in nanoseconds.
D123 §2b published WAIT and DURABLE at L0 only (T=64: WAIT 6.84 ms > DURABLE 5.35 ms). L1-L3 had
never been decomposed, and L3 is the rung the whole argument turns on.

  T=64 thread cycle = T * S_eff, median of 5 reps, configuration A:

           cycle      WAIT           HOLD_TOT        DURABLE          accounted
    L0     31.15 ms   11.76 (37.8%)  0.441 (1.4%)    18.43 (59.2%)    98.4%
    L3     17.98 ms   0.256 ( 1.4%)  0.011 (0.06%)   17.59 (97.8%)    99.3%

  L0 -> L3:  WAIT falls 46x.  DURABLE is flat (0.95x).  HOLD falls 41.5x.

⭐ AND THE ONE NUMBER A CANDIDATE SHOULD BE STATED AGAINST — LOCK-ATTRIBUTABLE TIME, which is
everything `logical` can possibly be blamed for, WAIT + HOLD_TOTAL, as a share of the cycle:

    L0   (11.76194 + 0.44130) / 31.146 =  39.2% of the thread cycle
    L3   ( 0.25570 + 0.01063) / 17.981 =   1.5% of the thread cycle

⇒ **At L3 the entire mutex — waiting for it AND holding it — is 1.5% of the time.** A mechanism
that deleted `logical` outright, made every acquisition free and every section instantaneous,
could not buy more than that. At L0 the same quantity is 39.2%, which is why shortening the
section works at first and stops working almost immediately.

⚠ This is a DIRECT MEASUREMENT of a share, not a forecast. Turning it into "removing the lock
buys 1.5%" adds a counterfactual: `durable()` is group-committed, so changing the arrival pattern
into it would change its own duration. The 1.5% is the honest upper bound on the LOCK'S SHARE;
what a mechanism would actually recover is not measured here and is not claimed.

⇒ **Time spent BLOCKED ON `logical` falls FASTER than the section does.** Threads are not piling
up on the mutex at L3; they have essentially stopped touching it. The gap that grows is
`durable()`, and at L3 it is 97.8% of everything.

⇒ This is strictly stronger evidence than the count, and it is immune to the section-1 blind
spot: a parked thread's wall clock runs whether or not its `try_lock` ever failed. Both witnesses
agree, and the one that cannot be fooled by barging is the emphatic one.

⇒ TWO INTERNAL CONTROLS AGAINST "PH_WAIT IS JUST ABSORBING SCHEDULER STARVATION ON A LOADED BOX":
  (a) L0 and L3 ran interleaved, in the same process, under the same load, with rung order
      rotated per rep. Starvation would inflate WAIT at both rungs. WAIT differs by 46x.
  (b) At T=1 (bench/ceiling_raw/11), under the same load, WAIT is 0.00032-0.00040 ms at every
      rung — four orders of magnitude below the T=64 L0 figure. PH_WAIT is not a general sink for
      descheduled time; it brackets the acquire and nothing else.
  ⚠ Neither control says a quiet box would report the same absolute WAIT. It would very likely
  report less. Both say the L0-vs-L3 CONTRAST is not manufactured by load, and the contrast is
  what the verdict uses.

5b. ⭐ THE OBJECTION THIS MUST SURVIVE: "BUT contended/op IS 0.908 AT L3, WHICH IS HIGH"
================================================================================================

It is high. The question is whether "high" is evidence of anything, and the answer is that a pure
mutex WITH NO HANDOFF FLOOR AT ALL also reads high at the same operating point.

`MODE=opref` runs a bare `Mutex` — no tree, no fsync, no second lock — at an operating point you
supply: hold and cycle, with the outside interval a SLEEP so the thread leaves the runnable set
as it does inside an fsync. ⭐ **Fed THIS session's own measured L3 numbers** (HOLD_TOTAL
0.01063 ms, cycle T*S_eff 17.981 ms, T=64), not the recorded ones — the reference in
bench/ceiling_raw/03 is parameterised by d123 §3b and would be the same two-instruments-two-
moments error this whole re-run exists to avoid.

  Ten draws (bench/ceiling_raw/30, /31, /32):  contended/op 0.525 - 0.738, median ~0.68
  Real ladder L3, median of 5 reps:            contended/op 0.908

⇒ **A mutex with no floor under it reads 0.53-0.74 where the real ladder reads 0.91.** So a high
collision count at L3 is the NORMAL reading for a lightly-loaded mutex under bunched arrivals,
not a signature of park/unpark. The real ladder is somewhat above the reference, which is what
bunching predicts — but see the caveat.

⚠ ⛔ **AND THE REFERENCE IS TOO NOISY TO QUANTIFY THAT EXCESS, SO NOTHING HERE RESTS ON IT.**
`calibrate_spin()` measures spins/sec on a box at load 25-38 and drifted 1.77e9 - 3.61e9 across
the ten draws — a 2x spread — so the modelled hold is not reliably the hold that was asked for,
and the printed `U_ach` is computed from the ASKED hold and therefore cannot audit it. The
reference establishes the ORDER ("a floor-free mutex also reads ~0.7 here"), which is all it is
used for. It does not establish "1.3x above reference" and that ratio is not claimed.

⇒ **THE CONCLUSION RESTS ON THE NEXT TWO ITEMS, NEITHER OF WHICH NEEDS THE REFERENCE.**

  (i)  THE COST. At L3 the whole of WAIT is 0.25570 ms of a 17.98 ms cycle — **1.4%**. Deleting
       `logical` outright, making every acquisition free, could buy at most that 1.4%. A 90%
       collision rate on a lock that accounts for 1.4% of the cycle is not a ceiling.

  (ii) THE CAUSE, and it is visible in a third number nobody had to go looking for. Bunched
       arrivals are the only way a lock can be idle 96.3% of the time and still turn away 90% of
       arrivals, and the thing doing the bunching is measured:

         f/sync (forks per fsync)                23.8 at L0  ->  32.0 at L3
         WAIT / HOLD_TOTAL (queue depth on arrival) 26.6    ->  24.1

       A fork at L3 arrives behind about 24 hold-times of queue, against a commit batch of 32.
       Same order, and it moves the right way. **The batch IS the queue**: an fsync completes,
       releases its batch, the batch piles onto `logical`, drains in ~32 x 0.011 = 0.35 ms, and
       every thread goes back to waiting on the disk.

⇒ So both witnesses are telling the truth and they are not in conflict. The collisions are real
and they are a DOWNSTREAM CONSEQUENCE of group-commit batching, not an independent handoff floor.
Outcome 1 required the remaining time to BE park/unpark; the remaining time is 97.8% `durable()`.

⇒ AND THE SERIALISATION DID NOT DISAPPEAR, IT MOVED. f/sync rises 23.8 -> 32.0 across the ladder
(bench/ceiling_raw/22): group commit is batching more forks per fsync, and the threads are queued
on that barrier instead of on the mutex.

⚠ WHAT THIS DOES NOT SAY. It does not say the fsync is irreducible, and it proposes no mechanism —
the pre-registration names flat combining, RCL, MCS/CLH and spin-then-park as prior art and
concludes "a mechanism here would die of prior art and I should not propose one". That still
holds. This section replaces a wrong attribution with a measured one and stops.

================================================================================================

CEILING-resume and CEILING-park-or-structure carry D123's scaffold. `src/branch/table_catalog.rs`
gates a `None` return on the stub level:

    let envelope = if stub >= 3 { None } else { self.envelope_bytes(parent.id)? };

That is a CAPABILITY ESCAPE. L1 also never writes the child's record, L2 leaks retired slots, and
L3 drops the parent's live-child entry (a GC hole). **A stubbed level is not a database.**
