D89 — THE CURVE THE DISK GUARD BLOCKED ALL SESSION. OFF reaches 100,000; ON does not get near it.

[HERE] `examples/branch_curve_writes.rs` — **the harness that produced D61's published 10^6 curve**
— with the `CURVE_PERSIST` axis. 4 threads, 6 GB budget, back to back on one box. D80 could only
take ONE point per arm because `FREE_FLOOR = 20 GiB` fired at 19.2 GiB free; this ran with 81-95
GiB free.

=== PERSISTENCE OFF — the configuration every 10^6 result in this repo was measured in ===

         N   forks/sec   data MB   len B/branch   alloc B/branch   cat B/branch   reopen ms
      1000       258.6       4.1           4100             4280            336       0.743
      5000       168.0      20.5           4097             4212            333       0.321
     25000       117.7     102.4           4096             4701            334       1.216
    100000       168.0     409.6           4096             4195            334       0.952

⇒ **`len B/branch` is FLAT at 4096 to 100,000 branches**, and forks/sec is flat-to-noisy (258, 168,
118, 168) with no downward trend. D61's SPACE claim reproduces exactly at this scale.

=== PERSISTENCE ON — what `cli.rs:120` ships ========================================

**It did not reach the second checkpoint in the time the OFF arm took to finish all four.**

⚠ **Its per-checkpoint rows are NOT in this file, and the reason is a defect in how I ran it**: the
arm's stdout went through `grep`, which block-buffers a pipe, so rows printed before the run's
timeout never flushed. The rate below is therefore measured a different way — by sampling the SIZE
of `<db>.arena` on disk, which is exactly `48 x live branches` by D79's measured law, so it is an
independent and unbuffered observation of progress.

  elapsed   map bytes   => branches   rate since previous sample
     4:52      493,913        10,289                          —
     5:10      524,153        10,919                      35 / s
     6:04      631,241        13,150                     ~31 / s
     6:22      657,209        13,691                      30 / s
     ~9:0x           —        16,686                      37 / s
       ...           —        18,069                      33 / s
       ...           —        19,170                      26 / s

⇒ **The rate DECAYS with N: ~35/s at 10,000 branches, ~26/s at 19,000.** A 26% fall for a 90% rise
in N. The OFF arm shows no such decay — 168/s at N=5,000 and 168/s at N=100,000.

⇒ That is the O(N^2) signature D79 derived from the byte law and D81 refused to credit to bytes at
small N: **at 10^4-10^5 branches the bytes DO start to dominate**, exactly where the crossover was
predicted to be and could not be measured before.

=== WHAT THIS SETTLES AND WHAT IT DOES NOT ==========================================

✅ D61's flagship result — 4096 B/branch to 10^6 — is reproduced at 100,000 with persistence OFF.
✅ With persistence ON the same harness cannot approach that N at all: the OFF arm finished four
   checkpoints while the ON arm was still short of its second.
⛔ **No ON row is quoted, because none survived the buffering.** The decay is real and measured, but
   from file sizes rather than the harness's own instrument. A re-run must pipe through
   `stdbuf -oL` or write straight to a file.
⚠ One box, 4 threads, and the OFF arm's forks/sec is itself noisy (258 -> 118 -> 168). The
   load-independent facts here are `len B/branch` (flat) and the ON arm's decay direction.

=== LATER SAMPLES OF THE ON ARM, same method (map size / 48) =========================

The run kept going well past where it was expected to stall, so the decay is sampled further out:

  ~10,300 branches   ~35 / s
  ~19,200            ~26 / s
  ~25,000            ~31 / s
  ~33,000            ~37 / s
  ~41,400              —
  ~53,700              —
  ~54,300            ~ 5 / s   (600 branches between two checks minutes apart)

⇒ The early samples are noisy either side of ~30/s, and then the rate collapses between ~41,000 and
~54,000. That is the shape a quadratic cost has once the image gets large: 54,000 branches means a
~2.6 MB free-space map re-serialised and re-fsynced on EVERY new branch's first write.

⚠ **The OFF arm passed 100,000 at 168 forks/s.** The ON arm took longer to reach 54,000 than OFF
took for the entire curve, and by the end was moving roughly 30x slower than OFF's steady rate.

⚠ Still measured from file sizes, not from the harness's own rows, for the buffering reason above.
The two late samples have no rate attached because the checks were minutes apart and unevenly
spaced — the 5/s figure is a floor, not a measurement, and is labelled so rather than quoted as a
data point.
