⇒ **OF THE 18 PAIRED DRAWS, ONLY FIVE FALL OUTSIDE EVERY DECLARED WINDOW** — `30_f1_paired_warm20k`
reps 4-8, at 28.2 s per rep. Those five give **median 1.97x, 4 of 5 above 1.10x**. One of them (rep
4, 1.03x) sits inside the falsifier's band. **An earlier draft of this artifact said "17 of 18" and
that overstates the clean evidence; the phrasing is withdrawn.** F1 is therefore re-run in
`bench/d123_final/51_f1_clean2.txt`, and the verdict below rests on that.

⛔ AND A DIRECTION ARGUMENT I MADE AND HAVE SINCE NARROWED. I claimed contamination biases the F1
ratio UPWARD, because interference hurts L0 more than L3 (L0 spends ~21x longer inside the section).
That holds for SEVERE degradation — warm=0's reps 5-8, where L0 collapsed to 303-2412 forks/sec,
produced the highest ratios seen anywhere (2.90-6.58x). **It does not hold for mild load.** In
warm=20k the contaminated reps 0-2 median 1.83x while the clean reps 4-8 median 1.97x, i.e. the
contamination slightly DEFLATED the ratio there. So "contamination biases upward" is a property of
severe degradation, not a general law, and it is not used here as a reason to trust a dirty arm.

⭐ THE METHOD FIX, WHICH MATTERS MORE THAN THIS PARTICULAR LEDGER. The rep-to-window mapping above
was reconstructed by dividing an arm's wall duration by its rep count — arithmetic on two file
mtimes. That assumes every rep took the same time, and **the reps we most care about are exactly the
ones that ran LONG because the box was loaded**, so the estimate is biased in the direction that
misplaces the boundary. The harness now stamps **each rep with wall-clock UTC in the raw matrix**,
so any declaration arriving later can be intersected with the data exactly, by anyone, without
re-deriving it. A derived quantity standing in for a recorded one is the same defect as section 0's
reciprocal, in a different costume.

⚠ AND THE `# other cargo/rustc/suite at acquire:` BLOCK IN EACH ARM OVER-REPORTS. It is a
`pgrep -fl 'cargo|rustc|verify-suite'`, which matches any shell whose command line merely CONTAINS
those strings — including this row's own polling shells, which carry
`export PATH="$HOME/.cargo/bin:$PATH"`. So entries there are an UPPER BOUND on what was building,
not a list of builders, and a reader should check `ppid`/`command` before counting one. This is the
same `pgrep -f` self-match that broke a wait loop in this row earlier; it is harmless here because
the block is a record rather than a gate, but a record that over-reports in the direction of "the
box was busy" would make a clean arm look dirty, which is the expensive direction for this artifact.
The trustworthy quantity in that header is `load_at_acquire`, which is not pattern-matched.
