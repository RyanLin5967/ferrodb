================================================================================================
0. ⭐ THE FINDING THAT MATTERS MOST: THE EVIDENCE W-B WAS BUILT ON IS AN IDENTITY, NOT A MODEL.
================================================================================================

D8's result, quoted into INVENTION-TRIGGER.md as what established W-B: "throughput = 1/serial_time
predicted 4,386 against 4,382.7 measured — 0.15% agreement across four runs."

That is division. In fork_concurrency.rs the two printed columns are

    per-fork ms = secs*1000/total          forks/sec = total/secs

which are algebraic reciprocals, so "1/per-fork-ms agrees with forks/sec" is true of ANY numbers
the harness could ever print. Checked against this row's own runs, where the same two columns are
computed the same way:

    T=1    forks/sec  244.7   1000/S_eff  244.7   ratio 0.999878
    T=8    forks/sec 1165.8   1000/S_eff 1165.8   ratio 0.999965
    T=64   forks/sec 5057.3   1000/S_eff 5057.1   ratio 1.000031
    T=512  forks/sec 4942.7   1000/S_eff 4942.7   ratio 1.000007

And the inherited residual is reproduced exactly as a ROUNDING artifact:

    measured 4382.7/sec -> exact per-fork 0.228170 ms -> PRINTED to 3 dp as 0.228
    1 / 0.228 ms = 4385.96/sec         discrepancy vs measured  +0.074%
                                       discrepancy from rounding +0.074%   <- identical

⇒ The 0.15% was the third decimal place of a rounded reciprocal. **It is not evidence that the
serial section is the binding constraint, and never was.** The trigger file's own lesson — "a tight
fit is the most persuasive way to be wrong" — is stronger than it knew: this fit could not have
failed. (Caught by the team lead on the throughput columns; confirmed here and traced back to the
inherited D8 claim, which is where it does damage.)

⇒ The section IS the ceiling, but that is established below by F1 and F6, on evidence that could
have come out the other way. W-B was right by accident.
