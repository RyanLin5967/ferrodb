2. THE REPORTER'S MECHANISM IS WRONG, AND THE INSTRUMENT SAYS SO
----------------------------------------------------------------

The hypothesis handed over, explicitly labelled a guess, was:

    on the trunk every page is private, so `cow_page` mutates in place, and a split failing
    part-way leaves the leaf truncated to its first piece with the remainder orphaned.

Instrumented by `d125_instrument_where_a_starved_insert_loses_rows`, which attributes every
failure without a hook in production code: the starving store logs the `PageType` of each
allocation it serves or refuses, and `alloc_for(BTreeLeaf)` is reached only from
`write_leaf_chunked` while `alloc_for(BTreeInternal)` is reached only from `internal_relink`
or the new-root tail of `relink_up`. Measured over the probe's own sequence:

    300 starved failures, 97 of them lost rows (235 rows total).
    refused allocation was a leaf page in 203 failures (0 of them losing),
                                an internal page in  97 failures (97 of them losing).
    leaf split had already completed in 97 of the 97 losing failures.
    lost rows by where they went: 235 into a subtree the failing call ALLOCATED and left
                                      unreachable,
                                    0 into a PRE-EXISTING subtree the failing call detached,
                                    0 unaccounted for by either.

⇒ **The stage the hypothesis blamed is the one stage that is correct.** All 203 failures
refused *inside* the leaf split lost **zero** rows: `write_leaf_chunked` allocates every piece
before it overwrites the first, exactly as its own comment claims ("Allocate before
overwriting, so a failure to allocate leaves the leaf exactly as it was rather than truncated
to its first piece"). Nothing fails part-way there.

⇒ **The loss is one level up.** The leaf split *succeeds and commits* — the original leaf is
truncated to piece 0 and the remaining pieces are written into fresh pages — and then
`relink_up`/`internal_relink` is refused its allocation, so those pieces are never linked in.
Nothing rolls the leaf back, because on the trunk every page is private and was mutated in
place. The hypothesis had the right family (orphaned pieces, no rollback) and the wrong
location.

The hypothesis's *premise* — "on the trunk every page is private, so `cow_page` mutates in
place" — is the part that is correct, and it is the whole mechanism.

4. THE FIX
-----------

`CowTree` gains a per-operation `WriteJournal` (`src/cow/btree.rs`) with two halves:

* **Undo.** Every page `PageStore::cow_page` hands back for in-place mutation
  (`copied == false`) is recorded once, with the bytes it held at that moment, behind a second
  pin so the restore can neither be defeated by eviction nor be refused by `read_page`'s
  checksum gate. Any error restores all of them, so the operation is all-or-nothing. `copied ==
  true` is deliberately not recorded: a shadow leaves the tree the old root describes untouched.
* **Deferred frees.** Pages the operation retires are freed only once it commits.
  `unlink_up` already kept its own frees to the end for D112's reason; that was sound inside
  `unlink_up` and not enough outside it, because `merge_right` calls `unlink_up` and then runs
  three more fallible steps. Rolling back to a tree that points at already-released pages would
  trade lost rows for reissued pages, which is worse than the defect. Both halves now live in
  one place, `CowTree::finish`, so they cannot drift apart.

Pages the operation *allocated* are deliberately NOT freed on rollback. Once the tree is
restored nothing references them, so they are a leak inside the branch's own arena, recovered
by `free_arena` — and `free_page` on an error path is precisely what D112 was. A leak is
recoverable; a page reissued while something still points at it is not.

This covers `insert`, `delete` and `merge_right` from the same mechanism, not just the insert
the probe exercises.

On the success path the change is observationally identical: `finish` runs before the entry
point returns, so `live_page_count` after a `delete` is what it always was. That is also why no
pre-existing test can see the deferred-free half — it is tested explicitly rather than assumed.

7. THREE THINGS THAT WERE NOT IN THE BRIEF
-------------------------------------------

**a. D113's end-to-end falsifier was about to go quietly vacuous, and this row caused it.**
`a_starved_allocation_never_leaves_an_unverifiable_page` inspects the tree AFTER a failed
insert. D125's rollback puts every page back before that call returns, so it reported
`220 starved failures (0 rewrote page bytes, 0 left a page compacted in place)` and failed its
own premise assertion. Relaxing the premise would have left it green, still asserting, and
blind to the defect it exists for. Instead the starving store now photographs the watched pages
**at the instant it refuses** — the moment the D113 defect exists — and the test reads the
photograph. It also now asserts D125's stronger property, byte-for-byte identity once the call
has returned. Both on every failure. The premise is live again: 220 of 220 failures rewrote
page bytes mid-operation, 32 left a page compacted in place.

**b. `merge_right` never allocates, so allocator starvation cannot reach its error tail.**
Measured, after two fixtures passed while proving nothing:

    terminators: 98 deletes, 0 allocations total, leaves 496 -> 400
     descending: 600 deletes, 1 allocation  total, leaves 496 -> 1
      ascending: 600 deletes, 1 allocation  total, leaves 496 -> 1

  400-byte keys give the content-defined chunker ~1.2 entries per leaf (496 leaves for 600
  keys), so a merged pair is ~1 KB against a 4060-byte page and never needs cutting. This is
  the same shape as D113's own "`leaf_put`'s exposure is real in the code but not reachable
  through `CowTree::insert`". The free-deferral window is therefore reachable only by failing
  something other than an allocation, and the starving store gained a read budget for it.

**c. `cow_page`'s shadow path never immediately releases the page it shadows** — reasoned, not
measured, and flagged as such. `cow_page` frees the old page only when `owned_by_writer`, and a
page in the writer's own arena was necessarily born after the writer forked, so `privacy`
returning false forces `reclaimable` false on the same live-children array and `free_page`
takes its `pending` branch rather than `release_page`. The immediate-release branch is
unreachable from `cow_page`. If that ever stops holding, a rollback would restore a tree
pointing at a page already back in the free space map, and the deferred-free half of the
journal does not cover it — the free happens inside the store, below `CowTree`.

⛔ **(c) WAS THE RIGHT OBSERVATION AND THE WRONG CONCLUSION. See section 8 — it is a real
defect, and "it only goes to `pending`" is not a defence.**


8. THE ADVERSARIAL REVIEW ROUND — one blocker and five test defects
--------------------------------------------------------------------

A fresh-context review of the diff. Every finding below was verified against the code before
being acted on, and every fix is fire-checked.

**FINDING 1 — BLOCKER, and section 7(c) above is where I talked myself past it.**
`cow_page` frees the shadowed source when the writing branch owns it (`store.rs`, and
`ArenaPageStore` in `branch/arena.rs`, **which is what the CLI ships**). `WriteJournal::record`
returned early on exactly that case. So the free was neither journaled nor undone, and after a
rollback the restored old root still pointed at a page the store had parked for reclamation.

My own doc comment named the error precisely while getting it wrong: *"a shadow leaves the tree
the old root describes untouched, so there is nothing to take back."* **True of the page's
contents, false of its allocation.** The undo list is contents-shaped; this hazard is
allocation-shaped. Skipping on `copied` was the right instinct applied to the wrong axis.

Section 7(c) reasoned that the page lands in `pending` rather than on a free list and concluded
it was harmless. The first half is correct — and irrelevant. A pending free is a *deferred*
release of a page the restored tree still references; when the pinning child is reaped,
`drain_pending_free` releases it under a live pointer. **A hazard that needs one more event to
fire is still a hazard, and "reasoned, not measured" was the flag I wrote on it myself.**

Fixed by making both stores **report** rather than free: `CowPage::retire_previous`, with
`CowTree::shadow` putting `previous_page_id` on the journal so it is freed at commit — the same
rule `unlink_up`'s retires already follow, one layer further down. This is D112's lesson and
D125's own deferred-free lesson arriving a third time.

**Why no test caught it, which is worth more than the bug.** Every test that inspects free
bookkeeping runs on the **trunk with no children**, where `privacy` is always true, `cow_page`
returns `copied == false`, and the shadow-path free never executes. The one test that does
exercise the shadow path counted **rows**. Neither covers the intersection, so "free the
shadowed source unconditionally" survived the whole suite. The new test
`a_failed_write_on_shadowed_pages_frees_nothing_the_tree_still_points_at` sits exactly there.

  Fire-check — restore the inline free and it fails:

    69 page(s) reachable from the root had been handed to free_page by an operation that
    then FAILED -- [155, 308, 315, 322, 329, 330, 329, 340]

  The other 8 tests in the module passed under that mutant, confirming the coverage gap.

Note the predicate this needed: `CowStore::is_page_freed` asks about the **pending-free log as
well as the free lists**, because a shadow of an owned page always takes the `pending` branch. A
check that looked only at free lists would have been blind on exactly this path.

**FINDING 2 — `finish` returned `Err` after the operation had committed.** That contradicts the
contract the rest of the function establishes and that the new delete guard asserts: an `Err`
from a write means nothing happened. Handing back `Err` once the tree had already moved tells
the caller to discard a live root — losing every row the operation wrote, which is this row's
own defect re-introduced on the success path. Now returns `Ok(new_root)`, with a `debug_assert`
so a failed release is still loud where tests run: every way `free_page` can fail there is a bug.

**FINDING 3 — the delete guard wrote `Ok(_) => break`,** discarding the successful attempt's new
root. On the trunk the root id usually does not move, so it looked harmless until an `unlink_up`
cascade moved it — after which every `leaves_and_internals(root)` was reading a tree that no
longer existed and under-reporting what was live.

**FINDING 4 — its non-vacuity premise paired two different attempts.** "This delete failed" and
"this delete retired a page" are about the failing and the succeeding attempt respectively, so
the counter was satisfiable by a run where every failure happened at `reads == 0`, before
`unlink_up` retired anything. Replaced with a depth measure: the deepest failure on a
page-retiring delete, which must clear 8. Measured **27**, against a 3-4 read opening descent.
The honest statement is in the test: an assertion cannot observe that window from outside, so
the fire-check is the proof and the premise is a floor.

**FINDING 5 — the release check was a NET page count.** `live_pages()` is
`next_free - free_pages.len()`, and this journal deliberately *leaks* pages a failed operation
allocated while it must never *release* one it retired. Those move a net figure in opposite
directions, so one leak cancels one release and the detector reports nothing while the defect
happens. Now per-page via `is_page_freed`.

**FINDING 6 — the reissue probe disarmed the louder detector.** `release_page` reports
`double free of page N` only while the page is still on `free_pages`; the probe allocated 4
pages after every failure and popped exactly those entries off, eating the evidence the
double-free panic depends on. Two detectors for one fact where one consumes the other — the
probe is gone, and the per-page check answers the same question without touching the free list.

**AND ONE THING THE REVIEW FOUND IN ITS "NOTHING HERE" SECTION, which was the most valuable
item in it.** The journal takes a second buffer-pool pin per recorded page and drops it with
`dirty == false`, while the real writer's handle drops dirty. That is safe **only** because
`release_pin_if_labelled` is `if is_dirty { store(true) }` — set-only. The obvious tidy-up,
`store(is_dirty, ..)`, is one line shorter, removes a branch, matches the plain reading of the
parameter name, and **silently stops committed pages ever reaching disk.** So D125 was correct
because of a buffer-pool property it did not state, own or test. Now pinned by
`unpinning_without_the_dirty_flag_never_clears_it`, in the buffer pool — where that edit would
be made, not three layers away in a caller nobody would re-run. Fire-checked: the tidy-up fails it.

**Review answers that were clean**, recorded so the absence is legible: no write guard reachable
from `insert`/`delete` bypasses `shadow()` (enumerated from the type — `NodeMut::new` needs
`&mut Frame`, so `PageHandle::write()` is the complete gate, and all 13 sites in `btree.rs` are
either a `shadow()` handle or a page that operation allocated); the extra pins cannot exhaust or
deadlock the pool (bounded by `2*MAX_DESCENT + 2 = 130` against 1024 frames, ~6 in practice,
resolved by `try_pin_resident` with no eviction or fault); rollback cannot restore a
mid-operation image for any page reachable from the restored root; and the deferred frees
neither leak nor double-free on the success path.
