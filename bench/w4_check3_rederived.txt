2. IS THE INSTRUMENT SOUND?  THE COUNTERS ARE. TWO OF THE CONCLUSIONS DRAWN FROM THEM ARE NOT.
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2a. THE COUNTERS CLOSE ARITHMETICALLY — on every row of both runs.
From the dispatch code (`src/pgwire/extended.rs`, `Statement::execute`): an ExclusiveEither or Dml
statement always reaches the exclusive path, and a SharedToday read reaches it exactly when it
stood down. So every row must satisfy, from five independently incremented counters:

      ann_exec  ==  (stmts - sh_att)  +  sh_stood

All 15 rows of the banked run close. All 15 rows of this run close. `ann_parse` closes too, against
the protocol's parse cadence (e.g. agent/extended 1604 = 1600 unique fork texts + 4 `ABANDON`;
agent_dml/extended 804 = 800 + 2 + 2; merge/extended 168 = 160 + 4 + 4; readonly/extended 0).
`mismatch=0` and `gap=0` everywhere.

⇒ The counter counts what its name says. This is stronger evidence than reading the source,
  because a double count, a missed path or a lost Relaxed increment would break the identity.
⇒ It also makes a real mechanism visible: A READ THAT STANDS DOWN BECOMES AN ANNOUNCER ITSELF.
  In agent/extended, announcements rise from 3200 (the forks) to 4765 — +49% — purely from
  stood-down reads falling back. The barrier has positive feedback. It does not change this
  verdict — the forks are still 3200 of those 4765, so removing the feedback entirely would leave
  two thirds of the exclusive announcements standing — but it means the measured fraction is a
  FIXED POINT of a self-reinforcing process, not a linear response to offered load.

2a-bis. WHAT THE FIXED POINT IMPLIES FOR A PARTIAL FIX — including W4 itself.
The feedback in 2a is not a footnote, it is the shape of the whole problem. Write the identity as
a fixed-point equation: with S the structural exclusive statements, A the shared-path attempts and
f the stand-down fraction,

      announcements = S + f·A          and      f = g(S + f·A)

because the fallback term IS an announcement. Any fix that removes announcements therefore gets
amplified by the loop: its direct effect is multiplied by 1/(1 - A·g'), which is greater than one
wherever the response curve g has slope.

⇒ THE CONSEQUENCE, AND IT IS NOT THE ENCOURAGING ONE. The amplification is only available where
  g' is LARGE. Section 5's curve is the measurement of g, and it is flat at both ends:
    * SATURATED (fork_every 1-4, f = 82-95%): g' ≈ 0. The loop gain is ≈ 1, so a partial fix buys
      almost exactly its direct effect — which, at f = 0.95, is almost nothing. Removing the parse
      site (25% of announcements in agent/extended) or the DML announcements moves a system
      already pinned against its ceiling.
    * STEEP (fork_every 8-64, f = 9-66%): g' is large, the loop gain is well above one, and the
      SAME fix is worth several times its direct effect.
⇒ So "W4 buys the 2.19% that is left" is the wrong arithmetic in both directions at once: it
  understates W4 where the curve is steep and overstates the value of any partial fix where it is
  flat. A lever's worth here is not a property of the lever. It is a property of where the system
  already sits on the fork-rate axis — which is, again, the one number neither run measures.
⇒ And it makes the measured 93-98% NOT an upper bound on what W4 would leave behind: it is the
  fixed point of TODAY's announcer set. Section 2h drives that perturbation directly.

2h. E8 — WHERE DOES THE FIXED POINT MOVE WHEN DML STOPS ANNOUNCING?
Pre-registered with predictions at `bf2b959`, BEFORE the first run. The perturbation is in the
WORKLOAD, not the engine: `W4_DML_EVERY` makes a DML client write every n rounds and READ in
between. Under W4 an admitted DML announces nothing and a stood-down one falls back and announces
— which is exactly what a SELECT does today. Same position in the path, same
announce-only-on-stand-down behaviour. ⚠ It models W4's ANNOUNCER SET exactly and its SERVICE TIME
not at all, because a read does less work after admission than a write would.

⛔ THE ARM THAT SUPPRESSES THE ANNOUNCEMENT DIRECTLY CANNOT BE BUILT, and that is a result about
the code rather than a preference. `ServerContext::catalog()` performs the announcement, the
reader drain and the mutex acquisition as ONE act with no seam between them. Patching out the
announcement also removes the statement's drain-wait and shortens its critical section, so
throughput would move together with the announcer set and neither could be attributed.

4,000 while `minted` climbs to 14,156: past ~8 ms the expiry rate stops being the limit, unreaped
branches simply accumulate, and the fraction plateaus at 21-26% with NO monotone trend inside that
plateau. The reaper self-throttles — `scan_once` chunks at `REAP_CHUNK=1` with a deliberate
stand-off between chunks, precisely so a sweep cannot starve statements. ⇒ SO THE HEADLINE IS A
CEILING, NOT A CURVE: however fast branches expire, the reaper caps its own interference at about
a quarter of shared-path attempts in a pure-read workload.

⚠ WHAT COUNTS ALONE CANNOT SETTLE HERE, stated because the flattering reading is available.
This is the only arm whose exogenous announcer is isolated and countable, so it is the cleanest
view of 2a-bis's loop anywhere in this experiment: 4,088 reaper acquisitions sit beside 103,110
stand-downs, a ratio of 25. But TWO mechanisms produce identical counts —
  (i) AMPLIFICATION: each stood-down read announces on its fallback, and those announcements turn
      away further readers, the loop of 2a-bis; or
  (ii) DURATION: one reap acquisition simply holds the catalog long enough to turn away ~25
      readers by itself, the fallbacks being consequences rather than causes.
Nothing in a count distinguishes them, and separating them needs a timing this experiment refuses
to take. The ratio RISING as the rate FALLS (71 at 105 reaps, 25 at 4,088) is weak evidence for
(ii) — overlapping windows would turn away the same reader once — but it is weak and I am not
resting anything on it.

⛔ AND THE RATE IS SYNTHETIC, which no amount of scale fixes. These points say what the reaper
costs AT A CHOSEN EXPIRY RATE. They say nothing about the rate a real deployment sees, which
depends on how often agent branches are abandoned and left to expire rather than merged or
abandoned explicitly. That is a SECOND unmeasured workload number, alongside the fork rate of
section 6, and it is now the other thing standing between check 3 and a verdict.

2e. THE HARNESS'S OWN FIRE-CHECK IS REAL. `tests/w4_standdown_instrument.rs` exists and asserts
what the artifact says it asserts. Re-run here: 6 passed / 0 failed with the feature ON, and 1
passed with it OFF (the `None` vs `Some(0)` assertion, which must run in both builds).


3. "A COUNT IS LOAD-IMMUNE" — THE INTEGERS ARE; THE RATIO IS NOT, AND THE ROWS DIFFER
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The harness module doc says: "The discriminating quantity is a ratio of two integers ... and an
integer is load-immune. Nothing in this harness is timed, and nothing in it needs a quiet box."
The banked artifact's section 3 already hedges this correctly in prose. It is worth a number.

The stand-down fraction is a sampled estimate of the DUTY CYCLE of `writer_active` — a ratio of
two durations. Both move under scheduler pressure and need not move together.

  E5, same arm, quiet vs beside 18 self-terminating CPU burners on an 18-core box:
    agent/extended     quiet 94.19%   loaded 90.19%    (-4.0 points)
    readonly/simple    quiet 11.88%   loaded 16.91%    (+5.0 points)

They move in OPPOSITE directions, which is exactly what section 2c predicts: the saturated
`extended` row is near its ceiling and can only come down; the `simple` row is a race won by
microseconds, and load costs the self-clearing reader its head start.

⚠ AND THE HONEST VERSION, which is stronger than blaming the other box. Three independent runs of
the SAME config on the SAME quiet box:
    agent/extended     97.81%   93.94%   94.19%     spread 3.9 points
    readonly/simple    10.50%   17.69%   11.88%     spread 7.2 points  (1.68x)

⇒ The `simple` and `ext_reparse` rows vary run-to-run by as much as the quiet-vs-loaded gap and by
  as much as the banked-vs-mine gap. I CANNOT attribute their 2x movement to box load, and neither
  could anyone else. What is established is weaker and sufficient: THOSE ROWS ARE NOT STABLE
  ENOUGH TO CARRY A MECHANISM ARGUMENT — which is precisely the pair the banked artifact's
  section 5 argument is built on.
⇒ The `extended` row is reproducible to within ~5 points across runs AND across boxes. The row
  that decides W4 is the row that holds still.


4. DOES A CONNECTION STAND DOWN AGAINST ITS OWN ANNOUNCEMENT?  NO. SETTLED TWO WAYS.
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BY READING. `parse_one` binds the guard to a local that is dropped when `parse_one` returns;
`CatalogGuard::drop` stores `writer_active = false` (`src/pgwire/mod.rs:288`). `execute` then calls
`read_catalog` and only afterwards `begin_read`. The guard is always released first. (It has to
be: a connection holding its own `ReadPass` and then calling `catalog()` would have `drain_readers`
spin forever on its own slot.)

BY EXPERIMENT. ONE client, so there is no second connection, and the reaper pushed out to a 1-hour
interval so it cannot be the announcer either:

  e3_self_reader   1 client, pure reader, 400 reads, 400 parse announcements per re-parsing proto
      simple 0/400   ext_reparse 0/400   ext_split 0/400   extended 0/400        ALL EXACTLY ZERO
  e3_self_forker   1 client alternating a fork with a read: 400 exec + 600 parse announcements
      simple 0/200   ext_reparse 0/200   ext_split 0/200   extended 0/200        ALL EXACTLY ZERO

AND THE CONTROL, so the zero is an absence of contention and not a dead counter — the same config
with a SECOND client added:
      ext_reparse 17/800 = 2.13%      ext_split 9/800 = 1.13%      (simple and extended still 0)

⇒ A connection never stands down against its own announcement, in any protocol.
⇒ So the brief's framing is inverted, though its conclusion holds: the `simple` row is NOT
  flattered by self-CONTENTION, it is flattered by self-CLEARING. The comparison is indeed not
  apples to apples, and section 2c's `ext_split` row quantifies by how much: 6.94% -> 88.31% from
  moving the same connection's own acquisition one round trip further back.


5. FORCING THE COUNTER TO BOTH ENDS — ONE ARM, ONE PROTOCOL, ONE KNOB
----------------------------------------------------------------------
A ratio that never reaches its ends is not calibrated. E4's first attempt used the reaper's scan
interval and could not move it at all (section 2d) — the wrong knob. The right one is the offered
fork rate, which keeps the arm, the protocol and all 8 clients fixed:

  agent / extended / 8 clients / 200 rounds.  `fork_every` = statements per fork.
    fork_every   forks   exclusive stmts   sh_stood/sh_att    FRACTION
        1          800        1600            761/800          95.13%
        2          400         800           1087/1200         90.58%
        4          200         400           1149/1400         82.07%
        8          100         200            996/1500         66.40%
       16           52         104            479/1548         30.94%
       32           32          56            447/1572         28.44%
       64           20          32            151/1584          9.53%
       96           12          24             73/1588          4.60%
      128            8          16             53/1592          3.33%
      200            4           8             17/1596          1.07%

  and the announcer-count axis, same arm and protocol, reaching the top:
    announcers of 8:   1 -> 83.43%    2 -> 87.17%    4 -> 95.50%    6 -> 99.50%    7 -> 99.50%

⇒ CEILING: 99.50% (199/200). FLOOR: 1.07% on the same dial, and EXACTLY 0.000000 in two
  non-degenerate configurations — readonly/extended over 3200 attempts, and the single-client arm
  of section 4 over 400 attempts WITH 400 announcements present. The last of those is the valuable
  zero: announcers exist, the mechanism is live, and the answer is still exactly nought.
⇒ The counter reads both ends and every value between, off a dial rather than off two special
  cases. It is calibrated.


6. WHAT CHECK 3 ACTUALLY LICENSES ABOUT W4
-------------------------------------------
LICENSED, and unchanged from the banked conclusion:
  * `begin_read`'s "only while DDL is actually in flight" is FALSE by construction (2b).
  * The deciding number is the `extended` row, and it is the row that reproduces (1, 3).
  * At the banked operating point — `agent`, every forker statement a fork — 93-98% of the reads
    the shared path already serves are refused it. A DML moved there by W4 sits in exactly that
    position with exactly that announcer set, because the `agent` arm contains no DML and so its
    announcer set is identical in both worlds. W4 would buy its win on roughly 1 attempt in 20.
  * The pre-registered "material" threshold is 1%. Every `extended` row in every arm is one to two
    ORDERS OF MAGNITUDE above it. That verdict is robust to everything in section 3.

NOT LICENSED, and the banked artifact does not say it, but the brief's summary of it does:
  * ⛔ "A KILL FOR W4's PREMISE" IS NOT LICENSED BY ONE POINT. The stand-down fraction is a
    monotone function of the offered exclusive rate and nothing else fixes its level. `fork_every=1`
    means four of eight clients fork on EVERY statement — 67% of all statements exclusive. That is
    a fork storm, not an agent workload; a real agent forks once and then does work inside the
    branch. Section 5 is the curve, and it is steep: 95% at 1-in-1, 9.5% at 1-in-64, 1.07% at
    1-in-200.
  * ⇒ THE HONEST STATEMENT: check 3 shows W4's payoff is governed by the fork rate, and that it
    is destroyed anywhere above roughly one fork per 64 statements (still 9.5%, ~10x the
    threshold) and survives only below roughly one per 200. The banked table samples the far end
    of that curve. WHAT IS STILL MISSING IS THE ONE NUMBER THAT WOULD CLOSE IT: the fork rate of a
    real BranchBench-shaped workload. Nothing in either run measures it, and without it check 3
    bounds W4 rather than kills it.
  * The reaper's contribution is unmeasured in both runs (2d) and could only raise the fraction.
  * The `Parse` site is a real and separately fixable cost, but it is NOT a rescue for W4: in
    agent/extended it is 1604 of 6369 announcements (25%), while the forks are the rest.

⇒ E8 SETTLES THE OTHER HALF, and it moves the verdict toward W4 rather than away. The measured
  93-98% is the fixed point of TODAY'S announcer set, not a bound on W4 (2a-bis). Perturbing that
  set directly: where forks are frequent, removing ALL DML announcements buys ~8 points and the
  lane stays dead; where forks are rare it takes stand-down from 85.74% to 12.45%. So W4 WORKS in
  the low-fork regime and is worthless in the high-fork one, and nothing else about it matters
  until the fork rate of a real BranchBench-shaped workload is measured.

⇒ SCOREBOARD ROW 3: the pre-registered outcome is **B — material, check 3 FAILS for W4 at the
  measured operating points**, with the amendment that the failure is a function of fork rate and
  the curve, not the single point, is the result. Outcome C does NOT apply: the instrument
  discriminates cleanly, closes arithmetically, and calibrates to both ends.
